## 1. At-a-Glance Summary

**Location:** Packet 06 — The Exodus Stair

**Current Table Baseline:** The party has reached the Summit Face at the concealed dwarven entrance threshold. The door has not yet been opened. The party is severely oxygen-deprived / altitude sick after completing the Shattered Moraine final ascent.

**Purpose:** Transition the party from exterior mountain survival into the upper interior of Stillpeak. The summit entrance is not a grand front gate; it is a narrow dwarven egress point used during the ancient exodus. The party begins at the dungeon's smallest point and descends through evacuation infrastructure toward the first wider interior landing.

**Expected Duration:** 60–90 minutes of play, depending on how much the party investigates the first refuge chamber, the exodus evidence, and the damaged descent.

**Encounter Types:** Threshold interaction, safe recovery chamber, exploration, environmental descent, resonance hazard, historical clue discovery, light haunting / memory echo.

**Key Mechanics:** Door opening under summit hypoxia, full release of exterior Mountain Toll / cold / hypoxia after entry and rest, Resonance Chalk discovery, damaged interior descent, resonance pulse track, mountain-attuned character advantage at the upper interior threshold, investigation of ancient evacuation signs, optional separation / positioning complications.

**Key Discoveries:** The summit door was an exit. Many dwarves escaped upward through this route long ago. The upper passage is evacuation infrastructure, not a ceremonial entrance. The lower kingdom was sealed behind those who escaped. The glowing mineral seams are post-tragedy grief-resonance contamination, not original dwarven lighting. The deeper route leads toward older halls and eventually toward the Bridgeward Way.

**Target Endpoint:** The party reaches the Exodus Landing, the first meaningfully wider interior space below the summit egress, and sees signs pointing deeper into the old dwarven kingdom.

## 2. Narrative Setup

### Scene Purpose

This packet begins at the summit face and ends inside Stillpeak. It should feel like a genre transition: the party moves from hostile exterior ascent into abandoned interior history.

Do not open Packet 6 with a large dungeon hub. The first interior space should be narrow, vertical, and transitional. The party has entered near the mountain peak: in topographical terms, they are at the smallest point of the interior structure. As they descend, the available space increases.

The summit door should not feel like an entrance made for visitors. It should feel like an emergency route designed to let people out.

### Design Intent

Packet 6 should establish a new question:

**Why were the dwarves fleeing upward?**

The party should not yet receive the full story of the queen's death, the immortal king, or the recent reactivation. Instead, this packet should reveal the first layer of interior truth:

- Stillpeak contains an ancient dwarven kingdom or seat.
- The summit passage was an exodus route.
- The flight was organized at first, then desperate.
- Some dwarves made it through the summit egress and escaped.
- The lower kingdom was sealed behind those who escaped.
- The deeper route leads toward something called the Bridgeward Way.

This packet should be dense with implication, not dense with rooms.

### Canon Arc Connection

The exterior climb taught the symptoms of the king's grief before the party knew the source. Packet 6 begins the movement from symptom to source.

The summit door is the threshold between the mountain's exterior grief-patterns and the buried dwarven history that created them.

Do not reveal the full grief-source yet. The party should first understand that a people fled, a lower kingdom was sealed, and the upper egress was used under fear.

## 3. Read-Aloud Scene Introduction

Use this after the party has reached the summit face and begins interacting with the concealed dwarven door.

The wall before you is not a cliff face in the ordinary sense.

Too much of it is vertical. Too much of it is aligned. Beneath the dirty ice and black stone, faint seams hold to one another with the patience of old craft.

The Tremorscope no longer points upward.

It points forward.

Then it answers.

A pulse travels from the device into the stone, and something inside the mountain replies. Not an echo. Not a warning. A recognition.

The ice over the central seam trembles in a thin white line.

Behind you, the summit wind claws at your backs. Your lungs burn. Your bodies have not forgotten the climb. But ahead, under the stone, there is air.

Old air.

Waiting air.

The summit is not the destination.

It is the way in.

## 4. Environment Overview

### Topographical Model

The party enters Stillpeak from its uppermost concealed egress. The structure widens as it descends.

Use the following spatial logic:

- **Summit Face / Door:** exterior threshold; hidden dwarven seam beneath ice.
- **Last Breath Refuge:** first interior evacuation exit chamber, built for recovery, accounting, and sealing the route behind escapees.
- **Broken Muster Niches:** cramped side alcoves for records, wounded, tools, and prayers.
- **Chain-Lift Void:** damaged vertical descent spine with stair remnants, lift chains, and maintenance ledges.
- **Exodus Landing:** first broader chamber, an old evacuation sorting point and future launch point for deeper interior exploration.

### Atmosphere

The upper interior should feel warmer than the summit but not safe. The air is stale, mineral-heavy, and preserved. Sound travels oddly: bootfalls are close and dry, but deeper pulses return late from below.

The Mountain Toll, cold pressure, and exterior physical timer pressure should release once the party is truly inside. The strange burdening effect that began beyond the Karagvorn Line applies to the mountain exterior; inside Stillpeak, the pressure changes from exposure and burden to resonance, memory, and unstable architecture. Summit Hypoxia may linger narratively until the party rests, but the first refuge chamber is safe enough to clear it.

### Interior Light — Failed Dwarven Lighting and Cursed Seam-Glow

Stillpeak's interior was not built to glow.

The dwarves lit these passages with practical infrastructure: torch sconces, lantern hooks, oil basins, signal lamps, maintenance lanterns, and ceremonial braziers. Those fixtures should be visible throughout the upper works, especially in refuge chambers, lift shafts, muster niches, and civic landings. Most are dead now: empty hooks, cracked basins, blackened wick-cups, cold sconces, soot fans, and lamp brackets still aimed at places where ordinary light once mattered.

The colored seams now shining through cracked stone are a later violation. Natural ore and crystal have been made luminous by grief-resonance after the queen's death. The glow is not intended dwarven lighting, not decorative spellcraft, and not a safe dungeon ambience. It is cursed energy leaking through old infrastructure.

Use the contrast deliberately: a dead torch sconce beside a green-white crack; a row of lantern hooks made useless by rose light bleeding through the wall; an oil basin long dry while yellow mineral-fire pulses beneath it. The dwarves built a functional kingdom. The curse made it beautiful in the wrong way.

The glow does not flicker like flame or behave like reflected torchlight. It pulses faintly with the mountain's deeper rhythm, brightening or dimming in response to tremors, memory-echoes, resonance events, and the approach toward the grief-source.

### Visual Motifs

- Scratches, boot marks, and hand-scars all oriented upward/outward.
- Emergency runes, not welcome inscriptions.
- Narrow dwarven engineering built for controlled escape.
- Dead dwarven lighting infrastructure: torch sconces, lantern hooks, oil basins, wick-cups, signal lamps, and cold braziers.
- Hewn stone walls split by old tremor fissures, with cursed mineral light visible through the cracks.
- Abandoned practical objects: survivor slates, splints, brace-poles, lantern stubs, broken wedges.
- Seals and warning marks pointing down, not up.
- Evidence that an organized evacuation became desperate.
- Ghostly multicolored ore and crystal seams casting yellow, pink, blue, and green-white light as post-tragedy grief-contamination.

## 5. Running the Location

### Transition from Exterior Systems

When the party opens the door and enters:

- The exterior Mountain Toll ends for the moment because the Karagvorn Line's strange burdening effect applies to the exterior mountain environment, not the interior evacuation works.
- The physical timer should stop unless the DM wants one final summit-face consequence before entry.
- Cold pressure ends once the door is shut or the party is fully inside the Last Breath Refuge.
- Summit Hypoxia ends after the party completes a short rest or controlled recovery in the Last Breath Refuge.
- The Tremorscope remains active and becomes more legible inside the mountain.
- Any mountain-attuned characters initially read the interior resonance more clearly than the rest of the party, but this advantage should be treated as the beginning of a long-term gradient that becomes dangerous closer to the grief-source.

The party should feel relief, but not victory.

### Mountain Attunement Gradient — Upper Interior Advantage

Two characters have become attuned to the mountain. In Packet 6, this should feel like a benefit before it begins to curdle in later interior packets.

At this upper-interior threshold, attuned characters are better receivers. Once per major Packet 6 scene, an attuned character may gain advantage on one check to interpret or anticipate resonance, including Tremorscope interpretation, Arcana, Religion, Insight, Investigation, Survival, or Perception checks tied to vibration, memory-echoes, unstable stone, or directional pulses.

Use this advantage to make them feel useful, not punished. They might sense a pulse before the Tremorscope visibly moves, know which fissure is about to brighten, feel a chain shift a heartbeat early, or distinguish memory-echo residue from an active hostile presence.

However, seed the future cost. When an attuned character benefits from this connection, describe a faint aftereffect: pressure behind the eyes, a hum in the teeth, a second heartbeat under the skull, or a brief sense that the mountain noticed them noticing it. Do not impose penalties for this in Packet 6 unless the party acts recklessly or the Resonance Pulse Track triggers a major consequence.

Design trajectory for later packets:

- **Early Interior:** Attunement grants clearer resonance reading and early warnings.
- **Mid Interior:** Attunement becomes intrusive: headaches, unwanted memory fragments, concentration pressure, and false certainty.
- **Deep Interior:** Attunement becomes a liability near the grief-source; the mountain and king's grief can push back through the connection.

### Door Opening Rule

Do not make the summit door a long puzzle. The party earned this threshold during Packet 5.

The door opens after any two meaningful contributions:

- Tremorscope aligned to the returning pulse.
- Ice cleared from the central seam.
- Dwarven construction logic recognized.
- Radiant, thunder, fire, or force applied carefully to the resonance line.
- A PC accepts a cost: fatigue, damage, lost gear, or holding position while others work.

Suggested checks:

- **Arcana, Investigation, Religion, or Tremorscope interpretation DC 15** to confirm the resonance seam.
- **Athletics DC 16** to clear ice and stone under summit pressure.
- **Dwarvish, History, mason's tools, or stonework reasoning DC 15** to identify the opening logic.
- **Group stabilization DC 16** if the exterior pressure surges before entry.

### Safe Rest Rule — Last Breath Refuge

The Last Breath Refuge is safe enough for the party to take an immediate short rest. This rest should fully clear the lingering exterior conditions:

- Remove Mountain Toll burden effects.
- Remove active cold-pressure consequences.
- Remove Summit Hypoxia / altitude sickness effects.
- Allow normal short-rest recovery.

Do not punish the party mechanically for resting here. They earned the reset by completing the Shattered Moraine and opening the summit door.

The rest should still be atmospheric. Use memory-whispers from the final escape to deepen the room, not to threaten the party. These echoes are stone-held memories, not ghosts or active enemies. Attuned characters may receive these whispers a breath earlier or with more physical sensation, such as words vibrating in their jaw rather than reaching their ears.

### Interior Pressure System — Resonance Pulse Track

Replace the exterior timer with a lighter interior pressure tool after the party leaves the Last Breath Refuge or begins forceful/careless exploration beyond it.

Start the **Resonance Pulse Track** at 0.

Add 1 Pulse when:

- the party makes a loud, forceful, or careless action in the egress stair
- a major check fails by 5 or more
- someone strikes load-bearing stone without care
- the party lingers in an unstable descent area while the Tremorscope is actively pulsing
- the DM wants to signal the mountain answering from below

At **3 Pulse**, the shaft answers. Reset the track to 0 after resolving one consequence.

Possible pulse consequences:

- The lift chain swings and forces a **DC 14 Dexterity save** to avoid being knocked off balance.
- Dust falls upward for a moment, then drops all at once, imposing disadvantage on the next Perception check.
- A stair segment shifts; one PC must make **Athletics or Acrobatics DC 15** or lose position / become separated.
- A memory-echo plays from the stone, revealing a fragment of the exodus.
- The Tremorscope vibrates hard enough to point toward a lower passage or sealed door.
- An attuned character hears the pulse inside their head before the environment moves; they may warn the party, but the sensation should leave a lingering hum.

This should create pressure without repeating the Shattered Moraine's full timed intensity.

## 6. Encounter Modules

### BEAT 1 — The Door That Was an Exit

**Map Zone:** Summit Face / threshold.

**Purpose:** Open the concealed dwarven summit door and transition from exterior pressure to interior dread.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Revealing and opening the door while still suffering summit hypoxia.

**Primary Challenge:** Act decisively without turning the door into a stall puzzle.

**Suggested Checks:**

- Tremorscope interpretation, Arcana, Investigation, or Religion **DC 15** to align with the returning pulse. A mountain-attuned character may gain advantage if their connection has not already been used in this major scene.
- Athletics **DC 16** to clear the ice seam.
- Dwarvish, History, mason's tools, or stonework logic **DC 15** to identify dwarven opening logic.
- Group stabilization **DC 16** if the summit face shifts before entry.

**Success:** The door opens. The party enters the Last Breath Refuge. Exterior timer pressure releases.

**Failure:** The door still becomes openable, but the party pays for delay: one character takes minor bludgeoning/cold damage, drops gear, or enters the refuge in worse narrative condition. Do not block access after the party has reached this threshold.

**Door Opening Read-Aloud:**

The seam does not crack open all at once.

First, the ice over it fractures in a single clean line. Then the stone beneath answers with a sound too deep to be heard only by the ears. It moves through your boots, your teeth, your ribs, and the Tremorscope shakes so hard it seems for a moment it might break apart.

The mountain exhales.

Not wind.

Air from within.

Warmer than the summit. Older than the storm.

The black seam widens into darkness, and beyond it stone steps descend into Stillpeak.

**Immediate Interior Cue:**

The first breath inside does not heal you.

It simply stops the mountain from taking more.

The air is warmer than the summit, but old enough to taste of metal, dust, and sealed stone. Behind you, the wind screams against the open seam. Ahead, a worked chamber waits in dim, wrong-colored light.

Your eyes adjust to two truths at once: the dwarves built this place for ordinary lamps, and ordinary lamps have long since failed. Cold sconces and empty lantern hooks line the stone, but the light now comes from cracks in the walls, where mineral veins glow softly in colors no flame should make.

Then you notice the marks.

Not on the outside.

On the inside.

Scratches. Boot-scars. Dragged metal. Handprints worn into the stone where too many people once pushed upward toward this door.

This was not built to welcome travelers in.

It was built to let people out.

### BEAT 2 — The Last Breath Refuge

**Map Zone:** First interior chamber immediately beyond the summit door.

**Purpose:** Give the party a safe recovery point, establish that the summit door was an emergency evacuation exit, and seed the first major interior lore through investigation and final-escape memory-whispers.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Recovering from the exterior climb, reading the room, and discovering what kind of route they have entered.

**Primary Challenge:** Understand that this was the last interior refuge before escape, not a welcoming entrance or guard post.

**Rest Safety:** This room is safe for an immediate short rest. Completing the rest clears Mountain Toll burden effects, cold-pressure consequences, and Summit Hypoxia / altitude sickness. The Karagvorn Line's burdening effect does not extend into this interior chamber.

**Environment Details:**

The room is squat, hewn, and practical. It is large enough for the party to sit, breathe, bind wounds, and spread gear, but it still feels like the top of a funnel rather than a grand hall. The summit door dominates one wall from the inside: thick stone, pressure bars, weather seals, and worn handholds all face outward.

The walls are cut dwarven stone, but old tremors and post-tragedy grief-resonance have split them with thin fissures. Through those cracks, the party can see veins of crystal and ore glowing in impossible colors: yellow like old lamplight, rose-pink like bruised quartz, cold blue like winter under ice, and green-white like mineral fire. The chamber still contains the fixtures of normal dwarven light: cold wall sconces, empty lantern hooks, a cracked oil basin, and soot marks where controlled flame once lit the survivor slate.

Use three dominant evacuation details:

- **Cracked stone benches** worn smooth by exhausted evacuees who made it to the exit chamber.
- **Broken water cistern channels** that once fed a small emergency basin near the wall.
- **Old handprints around pressure-seal levers**, showing where dwarves sealed or braced the route behind them.

A dark slate is affixed to one wall at dwarven eye-level. Its inscription is not a welcome message. It is a survivor record. If someone reads Dwarvish, the slate lists family names, presumably those confirmed to have made it to the summit egress and escaped.

**Key Object — Resonance Chalk:**

A cracked stone box beneath the slate contains three sticks of pale blue-gray **Resonance Chalk** wrapped in brittle waxed cloth.

Resonance Chalk was used by dwarven surveyors and emergency engineers to mark unstable seams, pressure doors, and tremor-sensitive stone. When used on resonant stonework, it leaves a faint line that trembles, brightens, or powders away in response to vibration.

**Suggested Use:** The party may use one stick of Resonance Chalk to mark a stone seam, door, bridge point, or unstable architectural feature. For one future interior puzzle, hazard, or route-reading moment, the chalk can grant advantage on an Investigation, Arcana, Survival, mason's tools, or Dwarvish/stonework check involving resonance or structural stress. It should not become a general-purpose magic detector.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Dwarvish DC 12 — Survivor Slate:** The slate lists family names rather than military units or noble titles. On a success, the reader understands this was a final accounting of people confirmed to have reached the summit egress, likely after escape became possible. The repeated family names make the chamber feel less like a checkpoint and more like the place where survivors were recorded before the door froze, sealed, or became unusable.
- **Investigation DC 13 — Exit-Side Layout:** The room's practical surfaces all face the summit door: benches angled toward it, worn handholds beside it, pressure bars operated from this side, and floor scuffs leading outward. On a success, the party identifies this chamber as an exit-side refuge, not an entrance hall. Travelers were not welcomed here; survivors recovered here after reaching the top of the escape route.
- **History DC 14 — Organized Evacuation Infrastructure:** The room's features are too deliberate to be improvised panic: cistern channels, wall slate, pressure-seal hardware, recovery benches, lamp hooks, and old emergency sconces. On a success, the character recognizes an organized evacuation system that was used under emergency conditions. The first impression should be discipline under collapse, not simple chaos.
- **Survival DC 13 — Exhaustion Patterns:** The benches, broken cistern basin, and worn floor show people arrived depleted, wet, injured, and breathless after climbing up from below. On a success, the character can tell this was a recovery point after the hardest part of escape, not a place where evacuees waited to continue upward.
- **Perception DC 13 — Pressure-Seal Handprints:** Old handprints, darkened palm-smears, and worn lever edges cluster around the pressure-seal mechanism. On a success, the character notices that many hands operated or braced the levers from this side, suggesting the route below was sealed, held, or locked behind those who escaped.
- **Arcana or Investigation DC 14 — Resonance Chalk:** The chalk is stored like a tool, not treasure. On a success, the character identifies it as a dwarven surveyor's aid for marking tremor-sensitive seams, pressure doors, and unstable stonework. If tested on a fissure, the chalk line faintly trembles with the mountain's deep pulse before settling. A mountain-attuned character may feel the chalk response as a hum in their teeth before the line visibly moves.
- **Religion or Insight DC 14 — Grief Without Malice:** The chamber feels heavy, but not hateful. On a success, the character understands the emotional residue here is not an attack or curse. It is the memory of people counting survivors, sealing a route, and carrying the guilt of those who did not make it.
- **Investigation or Arcana DC 13 — Wrong Light:** The room's sconces, lamp hooks, oil basin, soot fans, and slate-lighting angles prove that this chamber was designed for ordinary flame. On a success, the character understands that the glowing mineral seams are a later corruption, not part of the original dwarven design.

**Success:** The party understands this was the last interior refuge before escape and can recover safely. They may discover the Resonance Chalk and learn that the dwarves had practical tools for reading resonance and unstable stone.

**Failure:** The party still rests safely, but may initially misread the room as a guard chamber or supply alcove. Later evidence should clarify the evacuation function.

**Short Rest Memory-Whispers:**

During the rest, the DM may quietly whisper one memory to each player. These are final-escape memories held in the stone. They are not combat triggers and do not interrupt the rest. Each whisper should feel personal and incomplete, as though the chamber briefly mistook a living adventurer for one of the last dwarves who passed through.

Use one per player, or choose only the ones that fit the moment:

- **Whisper 1 — The Counter:** "Five from Hearth Dromun. Three from Keld's lower hall. Two carried. One breathing. Mark them. Mark them before the door freezes."
- **Whisper 2 — The Sealer:** "Hands here. When I say, push. Do not listen below. If they knock after the third bell, it is not them anymore."
- **Whisper 3 — The Child:** "Is this the sky? I thought it would be warmer. I thought she would come with us."
- **Whisper 4 — The Steward:** "Names on the slate. No titles. No ranks. Families only. If the king asks who left, let the stone answer for us."
- **Whisper 5 — The Last Look Back:** "Leave the lower lamps burning. Someone may still see them. Someone may still climb."

**Memory-Echo Boundary:** These whispers should suggest final escape, survivor counting, sealing the route, and guilt over those left below. Do not reveal the queen's death, the king's immortality, or the exact source of the mountain's grief yet.

**Attuned Character Cue:** If one of the mountain-attuned characters rests near a glowing seam or pressure-seal lever, they may receive their whisper a moment before the others. The sound is not louder; it is more internal, as though the words vibrate through their jaw. This grants no penalty in Packet 6, but establishes that the connection is becoming two-way.

**Spoken Cues:**

- "This room is not arranged to receive travelers. It is arranged to recover survivors."
- "Every practical surface faces the door behind you. Everything in the room assumes the next important act is getting out."
- "The old sconces are cold. The room was built for flame, but the light now comes from wounds in the stone."

### BEAT 3 — The Broken Muster Niches

**Map Zone:** Small side alcoves branching from the egress passage below the Last Breath Refuge.

**Purpose:** Show that the exodus was organized at first, then deteriorated.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Investigating cramped emergency staging alcoves after leaving the refuge.

**Primary Challenge:** Gather clues without bogging the packet down in too many tiny rooms.

Run these as quick modular discoveries. The party does not need to inspect every niche.

#### Niche A — The Record Niche

Broken slate tablets, bronze tags, and family marks lie scattered under dust. A dead lamp bracket above the record shelf shows where evacuation tallies were once read by controlled flame; now a rose seam behind the shelf lights the tablets from the wrong side.

- Investigation **DC 13:** These are evacuation tallies, not trade records.
- Dwarvish **DC 12:** Repeated marks indicate family groups, assigned routes, and status checks.
- History **DC 14:** The system began orderly but was abandoned mid-process.

Possible readable fragments:

- "Upper egress open by steward order."
- "Children and injured first."
- "Do not return below the Mourning Seal without geomancer escort."

#### Niche B — The Wounded Niche

Rust-dark cot rings, empty salve jars, cracked splints, and broken brace-poles remain stacked along the wall. A cracked oil cup hangs above the central cot, long dry.

- Medicine **DC 13:** This was emergency evacuation aid, not long-term care.
- Insight **DC 13:** The injured were being moved upward, not treated in place.
- Perception **DC 12:** Several braces were snapped or abandoned in haste.

#### Niche C — The Tool Niche

Door wedges, cracked braces, pitons, stone dust, and broken pry-bars cluster around a recessed shelf. The shelf is lit by a green-white crack that has split through an older row of maintenance-lantern hooks.

- Investigation **DC 13:** These tools were used to force or brace doors during evacuation.
- Athletics or mason's tools **DC 13:** The wedges were meant to hold pressure doors open against tremor movement.
- Arcana **DC 14:** Some tools carry faint resonance scoring, as if stone vibration traveled through them repeatedly.

#### Niche D — The Prayer Niche

A small relief shows a crowned queenly figure beside a mountain arch. Soot and scratch marks obscure the face. Below it, hundreds of small thumb-worn depressions mark the stone. A cold ceremonial sconce remains beneath the relief, but a thin blue seam now cuts across the queenly figure's crown.

- Religion **DC 14:** This was used as a prayer stop during evacuation.
- Insight **DC 13:** The figure was not hated. The damage feels desperate, protective, or ashamed.
- Investigation **DC 14:** The soot was applied by many hands over time, not in one act of vandalism.

**Important Boundary:** Do not yet reveal the queen's death. Let the party register that a queenly figure mattered and that her image became emotionally dangerous.

**Optional Memory-Echo:**

A pulse moves through the passage. For a moment, the thumb-worn marks seem newly dark, as if warm hands just left them.

A voice, far away and flattened by stone, whispers in Dwarvish:

> "Do not say her name in the stair. He hears grief before he hears words."

If an attuned character is present, they hear the sentence first as pressure behind the eyes, then as words.

### BEAT 4 — The Chain-Lift Void

**Map Zone:** Vertical shaft and broken descent infrastructure.

**Purpose:** Provide the packet's main physical challenge and show that the party is descending through damaged evacuation engineering.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Finding a way down through a shaft where the original lift system has failed.

**Primary Challenge:** Descend safely while managing resonance pulses, damaged stairs, missing platforms, and the Tremorscope's growing signal.

**Environment Details:**

The egress passage opens onto a vertical shaft cut through dark stone. A heavy chain hangs down the center, each link thick as a wrist. Dwarven stair segments spiral around the shaft, but whole sections have cracked away. A lift platform is visible far below, jammed at an angle, its iron braces bent like softened wax.

Maintenance ledges and ladder-rungs remain along the walls. So do practical traces of the shaft's former use: signal-lamp brackets, soot-dark maintenance alcoves, pulley inspection marks, and old lantern hooks placed where workers once needed steady flame. Some are intact. Some are not.

The chain does not sway until the Tremorscope pulses.

Then it moves a little too late.

Multicolored crystal veins run down the shaft walls like frozen lightning, lighting the drop in alternating bands of rose, yellow, and cold blue. Their glow crosses over dead sconces and lamp brackets that prove the shaft was never meant to shine this way.

**Suggested Checks:**

- Athletics **DC 15** to descend chain, ladder, or broken stair safely.
- Acrobatics **DC 15** to cross missing stair segments or unstable ledges.
- Investigation **DC 14** to identify the safest maintenance route.
- Dwarvish, History, or mason's tools **DC 14** to read lift operating marks.
- Arcana or Religion **DC 15** to sense grief-resonance in the shaft.
- Survival **DC 14** to coordinate spacing and avoid clustering on weak sections.

**Success:** The party descends to the lower landing with manageable risk and formation intact.

**Failure:** Add one complication: a PC slips to a side ledge, gear falls deeper into the shaft, the Resonance Pulse Track advances, or the party arrives split between adjacent ledges.

**Damage Guidance:** Failure should hurt less than falling off a mountain, but enough to matter. Use 1d6 to 3d6 bludgeoning depending on severity, or trade damage for position/gear complications.

**Resonance Pulse Consequences:**

Use the pulse track from Section 5. At 3 Pulse, trigger one shaft response.

Examples:

- The chain swings across the shaft; **DC 14 Dexterity save** or knocked prone / off-balance.
- A stair fragment drops into darkness and is heard too late.
- The lift platform far below answers with a groan and shifts several inches.
- A memory-echo plays through the chain.
- The Tremorscope pulls toward a lower side-gallery, briefly revealing a safer path.
- An attuned character feels the chain move inside their skull before it moves in the shaft, allowing a shouted warning or advantage on one immediate reaction check.

**Read-Aloud:**

The passage ends at open dark.

Not a room. A wound cut downward through the mountain.

A shaft drops through the stone, ringed by broken stairwork and old iron rails. A chain hangs in the center, link after link vanishing into shadow. Far below, something large and square rests at an impossible angle: a lift platform, jammed where it should never have stopped.

The shaft was built for lamps. You can see the proof in the dead sconces, the signal hooks, the soot-dark maintenance alcoves, and the empty brackets placed along the work ledges.

But the lamps are gone or cold.

The light now comes from wounds in the stone. Veins of ore and crystal run down the walls in broken colors: yellow like old lamplight, pink like bruised quartz, blue like winter seen through ice. The glow is beautiful enough to make the depth visible and wrong enough to make it feel watched.

The walls are marked with old instructions, capacity numbers, route arrows, and emergency cuts chiseled quickly enough that some strokes never found their proper depth.

Then the Tremorscope pulses.

The chain answers a heartbeat later.

Not from above.

From below.

### BEAT 5 — The First Echo of the Exodus

**Map Zone:** During or immediately after the shaft descent.

**Purpose:** Deliver the first true memory-fragment of the dwarven exodus without revealing the full tragedy.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Hearing or witnessing a stone-held echo triggered by resonance.

**Primary Challenge:** Interpret the echo while under descent pressure.

**Trigger Options:**

- Resonance Pulse Track reaches 3.
- A PC touches the central chain during a Tremorscope pulse.
- The party reads emergency runes aloud.
- The group pauses in the shaft and listens.
- An attuned character deliberately opens themselves to the pulse.

**Memory-Echo Read-Aloud:**

At first it is only vibration.

Then it becomes footsteps.

Hundreds of them, layered through the stone beneath and above you. Boots on stairs. Iron on iron. Children being hushed. Someone coughing too hard and being told to breathe quietly.

A voice barks numbers in Dwarvish.

Another voice answers from lower down, strained and terrified:

> "Bridgeward seals are failing. Do not send them back. Do not let the king hear the chains."

The shaft shivers.

For one moment, the air fills with the pressure of bodies climbing upward in the dark.

Then the sound vanishes.

Your own breathing returns, too loud.

For any mountain-attuned character, the last vibration remains after the echo ends: a faint rhythm behind the eyes, as if the mountain has not finished speaking.

**Interpretation Checks:**

- Dwarvish **DC 12:** Translate the spoken fragments.
- History **DC 14:** This sounds like an evacuation command structure, not a battle alarm.
- Insight **DC 13:** The fear is not of an invading enemy. It is of something below hearing or feeling the movement.
- Arcana or Religion **DC 15:** The echo is not a ghost in the normal sense; it is memory held in resonant stone. A mountain-attuned character may gain advantage, but on success they also sense that deeper echoes will not remain so gentle.

**Clue Revealed:** The dwarves were fleeing upward, and they feared the king or something connected to him detecting the evacuation.

**Do Not Reveal Yet:** Do not explain who the queen was, how she died, or why the king became immortal.

### BEAT 6 — The Exodus Landing

**Map Zone:** First broader chamber below the summit egress.

**Purpose:** End Packet 6 at the first meaningful interior hub and establish the direction for the next packet.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Reaching a wider evacuation landing that points deeper into the old dwarven kingdom.

**Primary Challenge:** Orient to the first larger interior space and choose what to investigate next.

**Environment Details:**

The Exodus Landing is not grand, but it is the first space that allows the party to spread out.

It contains:

- collapsed sorting rails
- route arrows cut into the floor
- broken crowd-control barriers
- spent lantern hooks and hundreds of dead wick-stubs
- evacuation tally boards
- sealed lower doors
- a collapsed side passage
- a central damaged relief or civic emblem
- cold sconces, dry oil basins, and dead signal-lamp stations from the original dwarven lighting system
- broad mineral seams casting uneven ghost-light over the whole chamber as grief-resonance contamination

Directional markers point to several old routes:

- **Summit Egress** — upward, behind the party.
- **Lower Holds** — sealed or partially blocked.
- **Royal Descent** — barred by old warning marks.
- **Bridgeward Way** — deeper, not fully open, but aligned with the Tremorscope's pulse.

**Suggested Checks:**

- Investigation **DC 13:** The landing was a sorting point for evacuees.
- Dwarvish **DC 12:** Read directional markers.
- History **DC 14:** The evacuation appears official, not improvised rebellion.
- Religion **DC 14:** Some warning marks have mourning-rite structure.
- Arcana **DC 15:** The strongest resonance leads toward Bridgeward Way.
- Perception **DC 13:** One lower door was sealed from this side after people passed through.
- Investigation or Arcana **DC 13:** The dead lighting fixtures and glowing seams do not belong to the same era of use; the chamber was built for lamp-and-flame visibility, while the mineral glow is a later corruption.

**Success:** The party identifies the Exodus Landing as a controlled evacuation node and understands that the Bridgeward Way is the most relevant deeper route.

**Failure:** The party still sees enough to proceed, but may mis-prioritize Lower Holds or Royal Descent until the Tremorscope clarifies direction.

**Ending Read-Aloud:**

The shaft finally gives you floor.

Not much comfort. Just space.

The chamber below is wider than the stair, wide enough for a crowd that is no longer here. Low rails divide the floor into lanes. Bronze tally plates hang crooked from the walls. Hundreds of dead lantern wicks sit in blackened cups, burned down to nothing.

This place was designed for ordinary light: lamp hooks above the rails, signal sconces beside the doors, oil basins placed where attendants could refill them quickly. All of it is cold now.

No flame burns here, but the chamber is not dark. Thin seams of ore and crystal glow from the walls and floor in uneven colors: a yellow vein crossing a broken rail, a pink shimmer behind a fallen tally board, cold blue light pooling beneath sealed doors.

Arrows cut into the stone point in several directions.

One leads back up: Summit Egress.

One points toward sealed doors: Lower Holds.

One descends beneath a cracked royal crest: Royal Descent.

And one, nearly hidden by dust and fallen stone, points into a darker passage marked with a single old word:

Bridgeward.

The Tremorscope pulses once.

The mineral seams answer faintly.

For those of you attuned to the mountain, the answer is not only seen. It presses behind your eyes.

Down there.

Not urgently.

Not kindly.

Certainly.

## 7. Player Curiosity Hooks

Use these to respond to investigation without revealing the full deep-lore too early.

### Why Was the Door Hidden?

The summit door was concealed to protect the egress route from weather, enemies, and perhaps from whatever was below. It was not meant as a public entrance.

### Why Did They Flee Upward?

The lower routes were compromised, sealed, or unsafe. The dwarves were using the summit egress because something below had made normal internal evacuation impossible. Some dwarves reached the Last Breath Refuge, recorded family names, sealed what they could behind them, and escaped into the outside world.

### What Was the Mourning Seal?

The Mourning Seal was a containment boundary or restricted zone below the egress levels. Do not define it fully yet. It should point toward later packets.

### Who Is the Queenly Figure?

A royal or sacred figure associated with the mountain and the old kingdom. Her image became emotionally dangerous after the catastrophe. Do not reveal her death yet.

### What Is Bridgeward Way?

A deeper route associated with a bridge complex, royal ceremony, or major internal crossing. It should become the natural path into Packet 7.

### Why Does the Tremorscope Work Better Inside?

It is listening to the same resonance that shaped the dwarven systems. Inside Stillpeak, the pulse has fewer exterior distortions and feels more direct.

### Why Is the Stone Glowing?

The mineral seams are natural ore and crystal made unnatural by grief-resonance after the queen's death. The dwarves did not build the interior to glow. They used ordinary lighting infrastructure: sconces, lantern hooks, oil basins, signal lamps, and braziers. The current seam-glow is a post-tragedy corruption leaking through the stone.

### Why Are There Torches / Sconces / Lamp Hooks If the Stone Glows?

Because the glow is not original. The old fixtures reveal how the kingdom functioned before the catastrophe. Their failure beside the cursed mineral light should make the interior feel contaminated rather than enchanted.

### What Is Mountain Attunement Doing Here?

Attuned characters initially read the mountain more clearly. They may gain advantage on resonance-related interpretation or anticipation checks in the upper interior. This is useful now, but it is not safe in the long term. As the party descends toward the grief-source, the same openness will become intrusive and eventually dangerous.

### What Is Resonance Chalk?

A dwarven surveyor's tool used to mark tremor-sensitive stone, pressure seams, unstable architecture, and resonance-bearing doors. It responds to vibration and structural stress, not to magic in general.

- An attuned character hears a low hum inside their skull at the same moment the rest of the party sees the seams brighten.

The party may take a short rest in the Last Breath Refuge. This clears exterior Mountain Toll burden, cold-pressure consequences, and Summit Hypoxia.

### Resonance Chalk

The party can find three sticks of Resonance Chalk in the Last Breath Refuge.

Suggested function:

- Spend one stick to mark a seam, door, bridge point, unstable wall, or resonance-sensitive structure.
- Gain advantage on one relevant Investigation, Arcana, Survival, mason's tools, Dwarvish, or stonework check involving structural resonance, tremor-sensitive architecture, or pressure-seal logic.
- It cannot identify all magic, detect enemies, or solve unrelated puzzles.

- The summit door was an exit.

- The exodus was organized before it became desperate.
- Some survivors escaped upward.
- The lower kingdom was sealed behind them.
- The glowing seams are post-tragedy corruption, not original lighting.
- Bridgeward Way is the next meaningful deeper route.
