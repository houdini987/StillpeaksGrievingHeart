# Scene Packet 3 — Cinderwatch: The Frozen Village

## At-a-Glance Summary

**Location:** 3 — Cinderwatch: The Frozen Village

**Purpose:** Show the human cost of Stillpeak’s grief, reinforce the town’s culture of preparation and burden-management, and deliver practical and emotional context before the ascent.

**Expected Duration:** 45–75 minutes of play.

**Encounter Types:** Exploration, roleplay, environmental tension, low-level combat, uncanny village event.

**Key Mechanics:** Cold, navigation uncertainty, tremor signals, encumbrance culture, environmental footing hazards.

**Key Discoveries:** Clues about the mountain’s sorrow, the meaning of the Karagvorn Line, and the selective pattern behind Cinderwatch’s dead.

---

## Narrative Setup

### Scene Purpose

Show the human cost: a community frozen emotionally and physically.

Introduce the Vault and the practical culture it created.

Provide the first clear examples that Cinderwatch’s survival depends on discipline, storage, and respect for burden.

Deliver actionable intel and a clean transition toward the Karagvorn Line and lower slopes.

### Village Overview

Cinderwatch is a frontier settlement built from stone and heavy timber, perched where the lower pass levels out before rising toward Stillpeak. For roughly two months, the village has reported bodies found standing upright, shadows locked into snow, and wandering dead that do not behave like mindless predators.

Population is reduced and withdrawn. Most residents remain indoors, watching from frosted windows.

### Village Layout

- **Lower Road (Entrance):** snow-packed road, a single flickering lamppost, travel signs that stop appearing after the first wave of horrors.
- **Town Center:** longhall, tavern, inn, general store; minimal movement; voices low; no ambient animal noise.
- **Upper Row (Near the Slopes):** smaller homes and sheds; the Vault sits closest to the footpath toward Stillpeak; tools abandoned and rimed with frost.

## Read-Aloud Scene Introduction

The road into Cinderwatch narrows between dark stone walls and heavy timber structures half-buried in drifted snow. No wagon sounds greet you. No dogs bark. No smoke rises in confidence from the chimneys—only thin, uncertain threads, as if even the fires here are conserving their strength.

Behind frost-blurred windows, faces withdraw as you pass.

The village feels inhabited, but not lived in. Every door is shut with care. Every sound carries too far. Higher up the road, near the rising path toward Stillpeak, iron-banded stonework and squat sheds cluster against the wind.

Whatever this place has become, it has learned to survive by holding tight to what it cannot afford to lose.

## Environment Overview

- **Terrain:** Packed snow streets, icy thresholds, rooflines loaded with drifted snow, narrow lanes between stone-and-timber buildings.
- **Atmosphere:** Cold, muted, emotionally heavy, with long pauses between sounds.
- **Visibility:** Usually clear within town, though blowing frost and drifting snow can soften edges and distance.

## Running the Location

Use Cinderwatch as a threshold chapter. The village should feel reduced, watchful, and burdened by accumulated grief. The town is not meant to become a sprawling quest hub; every beat should reinforce practical survival, emotional exhaustion, or the unnatural pressure radiating from Stillpeak.

The party can interact with public locations in any order, though the Upper Row and the Vault are the natural escalation points.

### Key NPCs

#### Erevan Holt — Town Speaker

**Personality:** exhausted, measured, practical; speaks as though words weigh something.

**Knows:** first wave began roughly two months ago; bodies first stood upright, then some later wandered; the town has thinned into fear and routine.

**Wants:** outsiders to solve the problem quickly without triggering panic, exploitation, or a final collapse of morale.

#### Torga Stonesong — Dwarven Mason and Vault Keeper

**Personality:** blunt, competent, protective of the town’s last safeguards.

**Knows:** the Karagvorn Line is moving slowly downhill; carried weight feels wrong near the slopes; dwarven lore calls it a stone-sorrow boundary.

**Wants:** the party to store gear wisely and respect the mountain’s rules.

#### Lysa Fen — Tavernkeeper

**Personality:** guarded, suspicious, grieving; keeps conversations short until trust is earned.

**Knows:** undead wander at night; they mutter apologies; her brother’s body is among the frozen victims.

**Wants:** answers, and a way for the dead to stop standing where the living must see them.

### The Karagvorn Line — Local Understanding

Locals do not describe a wall. They describe a point where the world’s rules begin to slip. Hunters say the air feels heavier and that it becomes hard to trust direction. Dwarves call it the Karagvorn Line—a stone-sorrow boundary where grief saturates the land.

Villagers do not claim it is sentient. They claim the mountain notices you once you cross.

### The Cinderwatch Vault

To survive the ascent, villagers constructed a heavy timber-and-stone vault reinforced with glacial iron bands. Adventurers, traders, and hunters use it to secure heavy gear and unstable magic items before attempting any climb near Stillpeak.

**Security:** reinforced lock mechanism (non-magical), two custodians, ledger tracking deposits.

**Reason:** extradimensional storage becomes unreliable near the slopes; weight becomes dangerous; lost gear is common.

**Use:** this is the party’s natural offload point; it teaches encumbrance culture without lecturing.

- **Investigation (DC 13):** Frost forms geometric patterns inside—like grief crystallized.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** Extradimensional items feel stiff; minor magic feels dragged.
- **Insight (DC 13):** The town built this out of fear and necessity, not tradition.

### First Undead Encounter — The Frozen Dead (Mournful)

Place this encounter near the upper row or behind the tavern. A single corpse stands upright, coated with thin frost. Its shadow is frozen into the snow like a black stain. When approached, the head turns slowly—not aggressive, but aware.

**Behavior:** slow, dragging gait; soft moan; reaches out as if seeking something; does not attack unless provoked.

**If destroyed:** collapses into powdered frost like shattered glass.

**If spared:** it wanders aimlessly, always orienting subtly toward Stillpeak.

- **Medicine (DC 13):** No signs of struggle; death does not match exposure.
- **Religion (DC 15):** This feels like resonance or anchoring, not classic necromancy.
- **Insight (DC 13):** The undead is sad, not malicious.
- **Arcana (DC 16):** A faint grief-based vector points upward toward Stillpeak.

## Encounter Modules

### Encounter Summary Box — Goat on the Roof

**Type:** Uncanny village event / optional clue scene  
**Trigger:** On arrival in town or after a heavy investigation beat  
**Primary Tension:** Is this merely an animal, an omen, or a meaningful guide?  
**Outcome:** Leads to a hidden trail marker cache and the Harven Soot topaz choice.

### The Goat on the Roof

In the stillness of the village, the party hears a loud bleat overhead. A mountain goat stands on a roof ridge, impossibly comfortable, with old route tags, prayer strips, and frozen scraps tangled around one horn.

When it stamps, rings of frost fall in neat circles. Local residents claim the goat once belonged to a deceased trail guide named **Harven Soot**. No one agrees whether it is simply an animal, a bad omen, or a lingering habit of the dead.

#### Read-Aloud

The silence of the village is broken by a single sharp bleat from above.

Balanced on the roofline of a snow-loaded building stands a mountain goat, broad-hoofed and watchful, as if rooftops were made for it. Tied around one horn is a ragged braid of old trail tags, cloth markers, ration wrappers, and a small carved charm. It stamps once.

A clean ring of powder-frost slips from the shingles and settles in the snow below with almost deliberate precision.

No one nearby moves to chase it off.

#### Checks

- **Perception (DC 11):** The horn-tags include old trail-marker cloth and prayer strips.
- **Insight (DC 13):** Townsfolk are uneasy, not amused.
- **Nature (DC 12):** The goat appears physically normal.
- **Religion (DC 14):** No clear sign of undeath or curse.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** The frost-ring effect is odd, but inconclusive.

#### Player Approaches

**Calm the Goat**
- **Animal Handling (DC 14):** Approach without startling it.
- **Animal Handling (DC 16):** Coax it down safely with food or patience.
- Grant advantage if the party offers salt, dried fruit, or rations.
- Impose disadvantage if someone climbs aggressively toward it.

**Climb After It**
- **Athletics (DC 13):** Reach the roof from stacked crates or awning.
- **Acrobatics (DC 14):** Move on the icy roofline safely.
- On failure, the character slips, slides, or drops into a snowbank, crate pile, or hanging sign.

**Study Its Behavior**
- **Investigation (DC 14):** The goat’s stamping and gaze are not random.
- **Survival (DC 13):** The cloth strips are tied in the style of old ascent markers.
- **History (DC 15):** Some tags are from a now-abandoned route tradition.

#### Trail Marker Cache

If the goat is calmed, discreetly followed, or properly interpreted, it leads the party toward a drift-buried old route marker and a shallow hidden cache.

**Cache Contents**
- **1 usable coil of rope**
- **2 charcoal hand warmers**
- **1 flawless topaz worth 100 gp**

The topaz is wrapped in a scrap of cloth marked with a faded household stitch-sign. If shown to Erevan Holt, he recognizes it immediately as belonging to **Harven Soot**, an old trail guide buried hastily during the first weeks of the crisis.

#### Harven Soot Topaz Choice

Erevan cannot pay gold for the return of the gem. If the party agrees to bring it back to Harven’s burial site, he shares guarded but valuable local knowledge:

> “The first dead were not random. The mountain seemed to take the trail keepers first. Guides. Stone-workers. Marker men. People who cut paths, carried loads upward, or drove iron into the slopes. We told ourselves it was chance. It was not.”

In addition, Erevan marks an older, safer initial approach on the party’s local trail map. The party gains **advantage on one Survival check made before crossing the Karagvorn Line**.

If the party chooses to return the topaz personally:
- **Survival (DC 13):** Find the burial site in drifting snow.
- **Religion (DC 13):** Perform a respectful return rite.
- **Insight (DC 13):** Recognize the quiet relief this act brings to the few townsfolk who know the grave.

If the goat bolts, one horn-tag tears free and the party can still find the cache with:
- **Survival (DC 15)** or **Investigation (DC 14)**.

### Encounter Summary Box — Vault Break-In

**Type:** Low-level combat / civic defense  
**Trigger:** After visiting the Upper Row or discussing storage with Torga  
**Primary Tension:** Protect the town’s essential stored supplies from savage frost-rimed vermin  
**Outcome:** Earn Torga’s trust and a materially useful ascent bundle.

### Vault Break-In

A tremor runs beneath the village, and moments later a nearby store shed collapses inward. A mass of frost-crazed scavenger rats boils out from beneath warped planks and split grain bins. These are not ordinary vermin. The mountain’s influence has made them savage, desperate, and unnaturally fixated on stored burdens: leather, rope, dried food, oils, wraps, and salt.

They surge toward the Vault because it contains concentrated stores of exactly the things the town depends on to survive the climb.

#### Read-Aloud

A hard shiver passes through the ground—brief, but forceful enough to rattle shutters and shake frost from the beams overhead.

Then comes the sound: wood splitting, something small and many-legged skittering in a rush, and Torga’s voice barking from up the lane.

“Not the Vault!”

As you round the corner, a half-collapsed supply shed vomits movement into the snow. Pale, frost-rimed rats—too lean, too fast, too fearless—spill over one another in a churning mass. They do not surge toward people first. They dart for rope bundles, leather wrappings, salted stores, and the iron-banded Vault door, clawing and screeching with frantic hunger.

#### Recommended Encounter

- **3 Rat Swarms**

These swarms should feel vicious and disruptive rather than attritional. Reduce hit points slightly if needed to keep the scene moving.

#### Rat Swarm Behavior

The swarms prioritize:
1. Exposed gear
2. Food stores
3. Leather, canvas, ropes, and bindings
4. Any creature directly blocking access

**Frenzied Gnawing:** If a swarm starts its turn adjacent to unsecured supplies, it can use its action to ruin or partially ruin them instead of attacking.

#### Environmental Features

**Vault Threshold Ice**
- Any creature that dashes across the threshold or is forced to move there must make a **DC 13 Dexterity saving throw**.
- On failure: fall prone.
- On failure by 5 or more: also slide 10 feet.

**Loose Supply Crate**
- **Athletics (DC 13):** Drag a crate into place as improvised half cover.
- **Sleight of Hand (DC 14):** Snatch and secure a bundle before the rats ruin it.
- **Investigation (DC 12):** Identify which scattered supplies matter most at a glance.

**Storage Integrity Track**
Start with **3 Supply Loss points**.
Each round that at least one unchecked swarm reaches unsecured supplies, reduce the track by 1.

- **3:** no losses
- **2:** minor losses, no consequence
- **1:** one rope bundle or ration crate ruined
- **0:** significant damage; Torga’s reward is reduced

#### Checks

**Before Initiative**
- **Perception (DC 13):** Hear the swarms before visual contact.
- **Survival (DC 14):** These creatures are targeting stores, not hunting normally.
- **Insight (DC 13):** Torga’s alarm is about preparedness, not property alone.

**During the Encounter**
- **Athletics (DC 13):** Shove a crate or barrel to block a lane.
- **Acrobatics (DC 13):** Cross the icy threshold without slipping.
- **Sleight of Hand (DC 14):** Rescue a rope bundle, oil flask case, or gear wrap mid-scramble.
- **Animal Handling (DC 15):** Momentarily redirect a swarm with salt, fire, or thrown rations if the tactic is plausible.
- **Survival (DC 14):** Predict which exposed stores the swarms will surge toward next.
- **Intimidation (DC 13):** Loud aggressive action plus fire scatters one injured swarm for 1 round.
- **Nature (DC 15):** These are mountain vermin altered by hunger pressure and environmental stress, not magical summons.

**After the Encounter**
- **Investigation (DC 13):** The swarms tore into load-bearing gear first, ignoring easier scraps.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** The frost patterns on gnawed straps resemble the geometric crystallization noted elsewhere in Cinderwatch.
- **Insight (DC 14):** Torga’s respect is earned by competence and triage, not sympathy.

#### Torga’s Reward — Ascent Bundle

If the party protects the Vault with minimal losses, Torga provides a practical reward package.

- **1 Dwarven Load-Strap**  
  Reduces effective carried weight by **15 lb** until the next long rest.
- **1 Complete Climber’s Kit**
- **6 Glacial Iron Pitons**  
  Once before the Karagvorn Line, if properly used to anchor a difficult climb or crossing, the party gains **advantage on one group Athletics check or one climb-related check**.
- **1 Heatstone Wrap**  
  Single use; one character gains **advantage on one Constitution save against cold or exposure**.

If the Supply Loss track reaches 0, reduce the reward at DM discretion.

**Torga’s Line:**
> “You don’t survive here by carrying more. You survive by knowing what cannot be replaced.”

## Player Curiosity Hooks

- Why does the goat’s behavior echo old route practices?
- Why does Cinderwatch treat storage and burden as moral questions, not just practical ones?
- Why do the town’s frozen dead and altered vermin both seem drawn toward Stillpeak-linked patterns?
- What does Harven Soot’s burial and Erevan’s guarded knowledge imply about who the mountain takes first?

## Escalation Signal

End the scene with a stronger tremor, a lengthened silence, or the Tremorscope pulsing slow and mournful toward the rising trail.

After the party secures gear, gathers testimony, and absorbs the town’s warnings, Cinderwatch delivers its blunt truth:

> “No one who crosses the Line returns the same. If you go up, do not linger. Do not trust easy paths. Stillpeak remembers its pain.”

## Rewards / Discoveries

- Practical ascent gear from Torga
- Harven Soot’s topaz and its moral choice
- Better context for the mountain’s selective grief-response
- An older, safer initial approach marked by Erevan

## DM Reference Sidebar

Cinderwatch should feel burdened, not busy. These additions work best when they reinforce three linked truths:

1. The town survives through practical discipline.
2. Stillpeak’s grief affects burden, route-making, and ascent culture in patterned ways.
3. Even the oddest moments in town should feel like expressions of local dread, habit, or unresolved memory.
