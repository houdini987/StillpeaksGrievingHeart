# Packet 8 Image Briefs — Throneward Descent

## Purpose

This file captures the five recommended Chapter 8 image concepts for later AI image generation.

These are not final image prompts yet. They are descriptive creative briefs written while Packet 8 context is fresh, so a future session can turn them into final ChatGPT/image-generation prompts without needing to rediscover the visual intent.

Use these briefs for Roll20 handouts, player-facing scene images, or ChatGPT-generated visual aids.

## Shared Visual Direction

All five images should match the established Stillpeak visual language:

- dark fantasy Dungeons & Dragons scene art
- ancient underground dwarven city / royal interior
- monumental stonework, but damaged and abandoned
- restrained green-white crystal glow in Chapter 8, with occasional pale blue, rose, or muted yellow echoes only if needed
- dead lamp infrastructure
- royal dwarven geometry broken by organic crystal intrusion
- no bright heroic high-fantasy palette
- no clean active magical city
- no modern objects
- no cartoon comedy unless specifically noted for the crystal mouse
- cinematic, table-facing composition
- should look like a handout the players can study for clues

### Party Visual Continuity

When the party appears, describe them as a five-person adventuring group consistent with existing character art:

- **Erny the Mountainborn** — large goliath paladin presence, broad-shouldered, armored, physically imposing, solemn, protective posture.
- **Bilbo Laggins** — smaller monk / nimble martial adventurer, alert stance, mobile body language, likely studying patterns or ready to spring.
- **Maelreth** — dragonborn warlock, arcane presence, visually distinctive draconic head/snout/horns, drawn toward crystal resonance but wary of it.
- **Alina** — human fighter, practical martial bearing, weapon-ready, grounded stance, often positioned as a rear guard or tactical anchor.
- **Sorin Nighthollow** — female human ranger, watchful and perceptive, wilderness-trained posture even underground; may be accompanied by her wolf companion **Frost** where composition allows.

Do not over-focus on exact facial likeness. Prioritize party silhouette, role clarity, and mood.

# 1. Throneward Descent

## Scene Role

Opening image for Chapter 8 after the party leaves Queen's Bridge and commits to the throneward route.

This image should tell the players: the wide city mystery is over; they are now walking down a royal route toward the source.

## Core Composition

A broad underground dwarven royal stair descends into darkness. The viewpoint should be behind or three-quarter behind the party, looking down the route with them.

The stairs should be wide, formal, and built to make visitors feel small before the throne. The walls carry royal dwarven signs, sigils, carved route markers, and dead lamp brackets in ordered rows.

But the order is broken.

Green-white crystal seams split the royal markings, push through corners, crack stair edges, and glow from places that were never meant to hold light. The crystals should look intrusive and wrong rather than decorative.

The Queen's Bridge should not be visible in detail, but the image may suggest a darker opening or distant arch behind the party to imply they have just left it.

## Party Placement

The five adventurers stand or move downward together:

- Erny near the front or slightly off-center, shield/weapon presence, protective.
- Bilbo lower on a step or angled forward, ready and curious.
- Maelreth paused near a glowing seam, visibly affected by the resonance but not incapacitated.
- Alina watchful near the rear or flank, checking behind and ahead.
- Sorin scanning the walls or route markers, with Frost close if space allows.

The party should feel small against the royal architecture.

## Mood

Somber, tense, final-act descent. Not action-heavy. The dread comes from the route itself.

## Avoid

- Do not make this a mine tunnel.
- Do not make it a clean palace stair.
- Do not show monsters.
- Do not make the crystals bright or beautiful.
- Do not make the route branch into a city crawl.

# 2. Advisor Records / Druid Advisor Seal Pair

## Scene Role

Lore-handout image for the Advisor Records beat.

This image should help the players understand that Dhurak Stonevein and Veyra Deeproot were not random monsters. They were trusted royal Stone Druid advisors with authority, access, and court standing.

## Core Composition

A cracked royal records chamber carved into stone. Heavy stone tablets line the walls like oversized book pages or civic record slabs. Some are cracked, some pushed outward by crystal growth, and some remain legible enough to show repeated formal names and seals.

The foreground focus is a wall niche containing two palm-sized stone-and-bronze ceremonial advisor seals.

### Dhurak Seal

A split mountain line braced by a hammer-shaped fault mark. The symbol should feel seismic, pressure-bearing, and associated with controlled breaks in stone.

### Veyra Seal

Branching roots descending into a crystal vein. The symbol should feel like roots, mineral memory, and deep stone interpretation.

The seals should be clearly visible and visually distinct, not tiny background details.

Crystal growth surrounds the niche and repeats small echoes of the same two symbols in nearby veins, hinting that these marks will matter later.

## Party Placement

Optional but preferred: show one or two party members close to the niche, studying it. The rest can be silhouettes in the chamber.

Good arrangement:

- Sorin or Bilbo crouched close, inspecting the seals.
- Erny standing behind, solemn and guarded.
- Maelreth looking toward the crystal growth, unsettled.
- Alina watching the doorway.

## Mood

Investigative, quiet, clue-focused. The image should invite players to study the symbols.

## Avoid

- Do not turn the seals into treasure chests or obvious loot.
- Do not make the chamber look like a library with paper books.
- Do not show Dhurak or Veyra in monster form yet.
- Do not make the clue too subtle; the seals are the point of the image.

# 3. Crystal Mouse in the Mining Decree Chamber

## Scene Role

Comic-horror image for the Mining Decree / Greed Spiral beat.

This image breaks up the lore-heavy middle of the chapter while reinforcing that the corrupted crystal near the heart is trying to imitate life badly.

## Core Composition

A royal decree chamber with rows of stone command tablets. The tablets bear royal seals and short carved order fragments, half-buried under green-white crystal growth.

Foreground focus: a tiny translucent crystal mouse standing on or beside a cracked decree tablet.

At first glance, it should look almost adorable:

- oversized ears
- tiny crystal whiskers
- small glassy paws
- green-white glow through its body
- curious, squeaking posture

But on closer inspection, something is wrong:

- jaw opens a little too wide
- tiny needle teeth or shard-like mouth edges
- crystal body shaped by imitation rather than biology
- a faint predatory glint despite its small size

The tone should be funny for one second, then unsettling.

## Party Placement

Show at least one party member reacting in the near background:

- Bilbo recoiling or leaning in too close.
- Alina lowering a weapon with a confused, practical reaction.
- Maelreth watching with concern because the crystal is imitating life.
- Erny too large and solemn for the tiny threat.
- Sorin/Frost alert to the wrongness.

The mouse should be small but clearly visible as the scene focus.

## Mood

Tiny jump-scare, table laugh, then horror implication.

## Avoid

- Do not make the mouse a cute mascot.
- Do not make it gory.
- Do not turn this into a major monster scene.
- Do not make the comedy broad or cartoonish beyond the absurdity of a tiny creature doing 1 damage to dignity.

# 4. Final Royal Doorway / King's Chamber Threshold

## Scene Role

Cliffhanger image for ending the night after Beat 5, or restart image before the final boss session.

This image should preserve the boss reveal while making the party feel that the source is immediately beyond the door.

## Core Composition

A massive sealed royal doorway stands at the far end of a ruined implosion threshold chamber.

The doorway should still be recognizably dwarven and royal: heavy stone, formal geometry, royal markings, dead lantern posts, old ceremonial framing.

But the edges are swallowed by green crystal. Thin green light leaks from beneath the door and through cracks around it. The light should feel like a slow heartbeat.

The foreground and midground should show the aftermath of the Crystal Tremorborn / Shardling fight:

- broken shard bodies on the floor
- small green light draining from fragments toward the door
- cracked containment lines
- twisted metal braces
- prayer marks and engineering symbols partly buried in crystal

Do not show the King, Dhurak, Veyra, or the inside of the chamber.

## Party Placement

The party stands before the sealed doorway, small against it.

- Erny should look ready but burdened.
- Maelreth should be visibly affected by the pulsing light.
- Bilbo may be closer to the floor, noticing the draining green lines.
- Alina should be weapon-ready, keeping formation.
- Sorin should be studying the doorway or listening, with Frost tense if included.

## Mood

End-of-session cliffhanger. The image should say: stop here if needed, because the final boss deserves a fresh start.

## Avoid

- Do not reveal the final arena.
- Do not show silhouettes of the boss monsters through the door.
- Do not make the doorway clean or intact.
- Do not make this an action scene; the fight is over, and the next horror is waiting.

# 5. King's Chamber / Crystal Boss Reveal

## Scene Role

Major final boss reveal image or Roll20 splash before switching to the battle map.

This is the largest, most important visual reveal of Chapter 8. It should confirm the lore visually without requiring a monologue.

## Core Composition

The party stands at the edge of a surviving royal doorway, looking into a vast warehouse-sized organic geode cavity.

The room beyond the doorway is not a normal throne room.

It is a round, jagged, crystal-shelled blast scar. The walls, floor, and ceiling curve together like the inside of a broken geode. The floor slopes inward like a bowl toward the center.

At the lowest point is the throne-engine.

The Mourning King is fastened to the throne by crystal ribs and mineral growth. He is inert, trapped, and vessel-like, not standing and not commanding.

Visual details for the King:

- crown fused into the crystal behind him
- beard threaded with mineral veins
- hands locked to the throne arms
- chest or torso lit by green crystal light
- expression grief-struck and trapped, not villainous triumph

On one side of the throne stands **Dhurak Stonevein**, the Fault-Tender:

- towering crystal-earth titan
- stone golem-like mass but more druidic and seismic
- glowing fault lines through his body
- broken mountain/fault seal motif visible somewhere on his body or beam anchor
- posture protective and violent

On the other side stands **Veyra Deeproot**, the Root-Seer:

- towering crystal-root titan
- stone/crystal body threaded with rootlike growths
- branching roots descending into floor and beam structure
- root-and-crystal seal motif visible somewhere on her body or beam anchor
- posture protective, possessive, preservation-focused

A green beam from each Druid titan burns into the King.

The beams should make the relationship obvious: the Druids sustain and protect him. The beams go into the King; they do not come from him.

## Party Placement

The party should be in the foreground or lower edge, small against the chamber:

- Erny braced against the pressure, perhaps one hand near holy symbol or weapon.
- Maelreth visibly affected by the resonance, green light catching his scales/horns.
- Bilbo low and ready, looking for movement paths along the bowl.
- Alina in disciplined combat stance, immediately reading threats.
- Sorin with bow/weapon ready, eyes on the beams or boss positions; Frost low and tense if included.

The party should not dominate the image. The chamber and throne-engine are the reveal.

## Mood

Awe, dread, moral horror. The party should understand instantly that this is not simply “kill the king.”

## Avoid

- Do not make the King an active lich caster.
- Do not show the King floating, standing, or commanding the Druids.
- Do not make Dhurak and Veyra look like servants waiting for orders.
- Do not add minions.
- Do not make the arena a perfect circle or clean ring map.
- Do not make the chamber rectangular.
- Do not make the green beams come out of the King.
- Do not make the scene too bright or triumphant.

## Future Prompting Note

When turning these briefs into final image-generation prompts, preserve this hierarchy:

1. Mood and story clarity first.
2. Canon visual accuracy second.
3. Party likeness third.
4. Fine decorative detail last.

For Roll20 use, generate these as cinematic handouts rather than tactical maps, except the King's Chamber may need a separate battle map if desired.
