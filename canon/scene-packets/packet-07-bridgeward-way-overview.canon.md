# Scene Packet 7 — Bridgeward Way Overview

## Canon Status

This file is the final-draft canon overview for Scene Packet 7 / Chapter 7: **Bridgeward Way**.

It is intentionally segmented from the detailed Witness Halls, Queen's Bridge, monster, and treasure files to avoid the file-size bloat problem encountered with Packet 6.

This file establishes the chapter arc, reveal boundaries, intended endpoint, and major story beats. Detailed room keys, maps, encounter mechanics, and treasure should live in separate Packet 7 supplement files.

---

## Chapter Purpose

Chapter 7 moves the party from the upper Exodus Stair into the dead heart of Stillpeak's dwarven city and toward the bridge where the queen fell.

The chapter's central promise:

**The party does not merely discover where the queen died. They physically cross the place where the mountain learned to grieve.**

Chapter 7 should reveal the emotional origin wound more clearly than Packet 6, but it should not solve the campaign mystery.

By the end of the chapter, the party should understand:

- The route from Exodus Landing leads through Bridgeward Way toward the heart of the dwarven city.
- Stillpeak was a functioning dwarven kingdom/city, not a purpose-built dungeon.
- The city is now empty and lifeless except for the cursed pulsing crystals and memory-bound spirits.
- The bridge was a sacred/civic crossing tied to a religious or royal procession.
- The queen fell from the bridge because of a tremor.
- The fall was witnessed and catastrophic.
- In the immediate aftermath, dwarves scattered in all directions: some to warn the king, some to reach the cavern bottom, some to preserve order, and some to pray or silence grief.
- The path beyond the bridge eventually leads down toward the throne room and a future confrontation with the king.

---

## What This Chapter Must Not Reveal Yet

Do not reveal the full deep-lore solution in Chapter 7.

Do not yet state plainly:

- that the king is effectively immortal
- how the king's grief fused to the mountain
- the mountain-heart mechanism
- the final solution for breaking the king's attunement
- the full reactivation timeline
- whether the king is villain, victim, or both

The chapter should shift the question from **what happened?** to **what did the king's grief become?**

---

## Geomancer Boundary

Geomancers are a separate order and should not be treated as the dwarven religious/civic authority behind the queen's bridge tragedy.

Chapter 7's bridge material belongs to dwarven royal, civic, and religious memory. Do not frame the bridge procession, the fall, or the immediate aftermath as a geomancer-led event.

Geomantic concepts may remain part of the broader campaign, but the Chapter 7 bridge reveal should not depend on geomancer failure, geomancer containment, or geomancer testimony.

---

1. **Bridgeward Threshold** — leave Exodus Landing and follow the Bridgeward route.
2. **Empty City Verge** — reveal the vast dead dwarven city from an overlook rather than running a city crawl.

## Beat 1 — Bridgeward Threshold

### Purpose

Transition from the Exodus Landing into the deeper route that leads toward the bridge.

### Tone

The party is no longer in evacuation infrastructure. The route begins to show formal civic and ceremonial stonework. Spirits become more frequent. The crystals pulse more intentionally.

### Key Images

- Bridgeward route signs become more formal.
- Emergency evacuation marks fade behind the party.
- Dead lantern infrastructure remains everywhere, reinforcing that the current glow is wrong.
- The Tremorscope no longer merely points; it beats.
- Erny and Maelreth's attunement becomes more intrusive.

### Suggested Checks

- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Bridgeward markers are official route signs, not later warnings.
- **History DC 14:** The route likely led to a ceremonial or civic crossing.
- **Arcana or Religion DC 15:** The resonance is stronger because the route approaches a major grief-memory site.
- **Survival DC 13:** The route is structurally unstable but passable.

### Spoken Cue

> Bridgeward Way does not feel abandoned in the way the Exodus Stair did.
>
> The stair was a wound people climbed out of.
>
> This passage feels like a vein leading inward.

## Beat 2 — Empty City Verge

### Purpose

Reveal the dead dwarven city without turning the chapter into a city crawl.

The party should understand that Stillpeak was a living kingdom, not a dungeon.

### Scope

This should mostly be a scenic overlook. The party may inspect the view, read route geometry, and gather impressions, but they should not freely explore the city streets in this chapter.

### Key Images

- Tiered stone districts descending into the mountain.
- Dark rooftops, sealed doors, empty halls, and dead civic plazas.
- Bridges and ramps spanning interior chasms.
- No animals, no wind, no ordinary life.
- Pulsing crystals and ore seams running through streets and buildings like exposed nerves.
- Memory-bound spirits moving in distant loops far below.

### Suggested Checks

- **Perception DC 13:** Notice faint spirit-shapes moving in repeated loops below.
- **Investigation DC 14:** The district was evacuated or sealed, not destroyed by invasion.
- **History DC 15:** The road geometry suggests the bridge was a major civic/ceremonial crossing.
- **Arcana DC 15:** The crystals pulse more strongly when the party watches them too long.

### Spoken Cue

> The city below is not ruined in the ordinary way.
>
> It is too intact for that.
>
> Streets still hold their lines. Doorways still face each other. Bridges still connect districts that no living foot has crossed in ages.
>
> But nothing lives there.
>
> Only the crystals move.

## Beat 3 — Witness Halls

### Purpose

Provide a compact playable dungeon/chamber before the bridge.

The Witness Halls should prepare the party emotionally and mechanically for the Queen's Bridge without fully revealing the tragedy before the bridge itself.

### Scale

4–5 keyed areas.

The Witness Halls should include:

- a minor spirit/social scene
- signs of religious or civic procession
- evidence that the bridge was sacred or ceremonial
- growing spirit density
- a major grief-crystal elemental encounter using the Witness-Hall Grief-Crystal Animus from `packet-07-monsters.canon.md`

### Design Notes

The Witness Halls are not geomancer chambers. They are dwarven civic/religious spaces associated with witnessing, procession, mourning, and bridge approach.

The large grief-crystal creature is not a guardian. It is a malignant resonance growth that has taken on motion because the area is saturated with grief-energy.

### Suggested Area Structure

1. **Processional Entry** — carved route hall and dust-rotted ceremonial remnants.
2. **Hall of Witness Niches** — minor spirit/social scene; figures relive watching or preparing for the procession.
3. **Gallery of Covered Faces** — queenly reliefs are veiled, soot-covered, or unfinished; her memory is loved but dangerous.
4. **Crystal Growth Chamber** — the Witness-Hall Grief-Crystal Animus awakens.
5. **Last Arch Before the Span** — final overlook onto the Queen's Bridge.

## Beat 4 — Queen's Bridge

### Purpose

Major Chapter 7 set piece.

The party witnesses the queen's fall in layered spirit-scenes while crossing the bridge where it happened. The bridge is physically dangerous, spiritually saturated, and occupied by a foreign ancient predator: **The Hollow Below**.

### Core Reveal

The queen fell because of a tremor.

This should be unambiguous. The bridge memory must not imply assassination, betrayal, sabotage, or enemy attack.

The tragedy was accidental, witnessed, sacredly catastrophic, and immediately connected to the king's grief.

### Spirit-Scene Structure

The bridge does not reveal the tragedy all at once. Different moments play out at different points on or near the span.

#### Scene 1 — Before the Fall: Religious / Civic Procession

The party sees the queen crossing the bridge as part of a sacred or royal procession.

Use elements such as:

- attendants
- guards
- priests or religious officiants
- banner-bearers
- ceremonial lanterns
- musicians or bell-ringers
- onlookers positioned along the bridge or far-side threshold

The mood is solemn, formal, and reverent, not fearful.

**Spoken Cue:**

> The first spirits appear not in panic, but in order.
>
> A procession crosses the bridge: lanterns, banners, bowed heads, armored guards, robed attendants, and at their center a queen in pale ceremonial cloth.
>
> No one is fleeing.
>
> Not yet.

#### Scene 2 — The Tremor

The mountain shifts suddenly.

The bridge lurches. The queen loses footing or the stone gives way beneath her. The fall must read as geologic accident.

**Spoken Cue:**

> The tremor comes without warning.
>
> Not blades. Not arrows. Not betrayal.
>
> The mountain moves.
>
> The bridge drops beneath her feet.

#### Scene 3 — Failed Rescue

Guards and attendants reach for her. Someone catches cloth, jewelry, sleeve, or hand too late. Ropes are called for. The bridge fractures again.

**Spoken Cue:**

> Hands reach for her.
>
> One catches torn cloth. One catches air. One guard throws himself to the stone so hard his armor sparks against the bridge.
>
> Someone screams for rope.
>
> Someone else is already looking down, knowing the fall is too deep.

#### Scene 4 — Immediate Aftermath

The aftermath scatters in all directions.

The party sees dwarves respond in urgent, conflicting ways:

- some rush toward the throne to warn the king
- some race down toward lower levels, trying to reach the cavern bottom
- some remain at the bridge, shouting orders or praying
- some try to silence grief or prevent others from speaking the queen's name
- some freeze as witnesses, unable to move

**Spoken Cue:**

> Then order breaks.
>
> Dwarves scatter in every direction.
>
> Some run toward the deep royal road, shouting that the king must be told.
>
> Some race for lower stairs with ropes and lanterns, though the dark below the bridge has already swallowed the light.
>
> Some stay where they are, praying, ordering, screaming, or simply staring down.
>
> The fall is over.
>
> The catastrophe is only beginning.

### Encounter Trigger

As the party approaches the reenactment, the spirits begin to notice them.

The spirits turn to look. The crystals lining the bridge flare. Before the party can retreat or race across the bridge, initiative begins.

The Hollow Below awakens from beneath the span. Two Crystal Shardlings emerge from far-side crystal formations only when The Hollow Below activates.

Use the monster stat blocks and encounter package in `packet-07-monsters.canon.md`.

### Bridge Threat Model

The Queen's Bridge scene should be memory-first, with combat and hazards supporting the reenactment.

Primary pressures:

- The tragedy replay draws the party forward.
- The bridge tremors and fractures.
- The Hollow Below restrains and pulls creatures toward edges.
- Two Crystal Shardlings complicate far-side movement.
- The party must survive and cross.

Do not let the fight erase the reveal. The bridge memory is the centerpiece.

## Beat 5 — Far-Side Mourning Threshold

### Purpose

Provide the short post-bridge scene that ends Chapter 7 and points toward Chapter 8.

This scene should not become a full dungeon. It should give the party enough to understand the next direction: eventually downward toward the throne room and confrontation with the king.

### Tone

Immediate aftermath, royal grief, and directionality.

The party has crossed the place where the queen fell. The far side shows how those present reacted in the first moments after the catastrophe.

### Key Images

- Broken procession relics carried from the bridge.
- Torn ceremonial banners.
- Dropped lanterns and cracked prayer beads.
- Route marker toward a throneward descent, royal deepway, or similar lower royal route.
- Spirits rushing in different directions, still trapped in their urgent first responses.
- A spirit clutching torn white procession cloth and running deeper to warn the king.

### Suggested Checks

- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Read a marker indicating a lower royal/throneward route.
- **History DC 14:** Understand that the far-side passages include royal/civic routes, not common residential streets.
- **Religion DC 14:** Recognize that several objects were dropped during a sacred or formal procession.
- **Insight DC 13:** Understand that the spirits are reliving immediate aftermath, not guarding the passage.
- **Perception DC 13:** Notice several spirits rushing downward with repeated phrases about warning the king or reaching the cavern floor.

### Spirit Fragments

Use these as passing fragments, not full conversations:

> "Tell the king—no, not like that—do not let him hear it from the bridge."

> "Ropes. Lower ropes. She fell below the lantern reach."

> "No one goes alone. The cavern is too deep."

> "Throneward. Someone must tell him. Someone must survive telling him."

> "Do not say her name while running. Do not carry grief before the throne."

### Chapter Endpoint Read-Aloud

Use or adapt this to close the packet:

> The last spirit does not flee toward the bridge.
>
> He runs deeper.
>
> Down a stair marked by a cracked royal sigil, clutching a torn strip of white procession cloth in both hands.
>
> "Throneward," he whispers, though no breath moves through him. "Someone must tell him. Someone must survive telling him."
>
> The spirit vanishes into the dark below.
>
> The crystals along the stair answer with a slow pulse.
>
> Not a direction.
>
> An invitation.
>
> Or a warning.

## Chapter Endpoint

Chapter 7 ends after the party crosses the Queen's Bridge and experiences one short far-side scene.

The party should end the chapter knowing:

- the queen fell from the bridge because of a tremor
- the event was witnessed during a sacred/civic procession
- the immediate aftermath scattered dwarves toward the throne, toward the cavern bottom, and into panic/mourning
- the path ahead eventually leads downward toward the throne room and a confrontation with the king

The chapter should not continue into the throneward descent or throne room. Save those for Chapter 8.

---

## Design Cautions

- Do not make the empty city a broad exploration crawl.
- Do not introduce geomancer-led explanations in Chapter 7.
- Do not reveal the final grief-source solution.
- Do not reveal the king's immortality yet.
- Do not let the Hollow Below become the meaning of the bridge; it is an opportunistic tenant, not the source wound.
- Do not make the queen's fall ambiguous. The tremor caused it.
