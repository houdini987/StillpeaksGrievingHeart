# Scene Packet 6 Encounter Supplement — Ghosts of the Exodus Stair

## Canon Status

This file is a canon encounter supplement for `canon/scene-packets/packet-06-the-exodus-stair.canon.md`.

It defines four benign-unless-attacked ghostly dwarf encounters for Chapter 6 / Packet 6: The Exodus Stair.

These spirits are not ordinary dungeon enemies. They are dwarves who did not escape in time, now caught in the king's grief and forced to repeat fragments of the ancient exodus. Their default state is haunting, not hunting.

If the party treats them with patience, respect, curiosity, or aid, they provide clues, emotional context, or safe-passage benefits. If the party attacks them, disrupts their loops violently, or mocks their suffering, the spirits defend themselves through grief-resonance.

---

## Encounter Tone Rule

Do not present these spirits as default combat targets.

Their purpose is to teach the party that Stillpeak's dead are not automatically enemies. Some are prisoners of the king's unresolved grief. Violence against them should feel like a moral and practical mistake, not a normal tactical choice.

If combat occurs, the spirits should be unsettling but not built as major boss threats. Their danger should come from resonance backlash, positional complications, and escalating the mountain's response.

---

## Shared Spirit Traits

The following traits apply to all four spirits unless a stat block says otherwise.

**Undead Nature.** The spirit does not require air, food, drink, or sleep.

**Loop-Bound.** Until interacted with, the spirit repeats one fragment of the exodus. A loop-bound spirit does not pursue the party unless attacked or violently disrupted.

**Benign Unless Attacked.** The spirit is not hostile by default. If attacked, mocked cruelly, turned as undead, or forcibly disrupted, it becomes hostile for 1 minute or until the party disengages and ceases aggression.

**Grief-Resonant.** The spirit is vulnerable to thunder damage. When it takes thunder damage, the Resonance Pulse Track advances by 1 unless the DM decides the thunder was carefully controlled or narratively appropriate.

**Release, Not Death.** If reduced to 0 hit points through respectful resolution, ritual aid, or completion of its loop, the spirit fades peacefully. If reduced to 0 hit points through violence, it collapses into a grief pulse and advances the Resonance Pulse Track by 1.

## Encounter 1 — The Ledger Keeper

### Summary

A translucent dwarven record-keeper stands at the survivor slate, endlessly trying to mark names with a stylus that no longer touches stone.

He is repeating the last accounting of families who reached the summit egress. He is not guarding treasure or blocking the party. He is trying to finish a list that grief will not let him finish.

### Default Behavior

The Ledger Keeper ignores the party at first. He reads names under his breath, pauses at missing entries, then begins again. His stylus passes through the slate with a dry scraping sound even though no mark appears.

### Interaction Options

- A character who speaks Dwarvish may read names aloud with him.
- A character may use Investigation, History, or Insight to determine that he is not counting soldiers or nobles, but families.
- A character may place a hand near the slate, offer a name, or help him complete the final tally.

### Suggested Checks

- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Understand that the list is a survivor record.
- **Insight DC 13:** Realize the spirit is distressed by missing names, not hostile.
- **History DC 14:** Identify the accounting process as civic evacuation practice, not military command.
- **Religion DC 14:** Help the spirit release one name without claiming to know the final fate of all missing dwarves.

### Success

The Ledger Keeper briefly becomes aware of the party and reveals one clue:

> "Names on stone. Families only. If the king asks who left, let the stone answer."

He may also grant advantage on one later Dwarvish, History, or Investigation check involving evacuation records in Packet 6.

### If Attacked

The survivor slate flares with white script. Names appear too quickly to read, then burn out. The Ledger Keeper defends himself as a grief-bound record spirit.

### Simple Stat Block — Ledger Keeper

**Medium undead, neutral**  
**AC:** 13  
**HP:** 22  
**Speed:** 0 ft., fly 20 ft. (hover)  
**STR:** 6 (-2) **DEX:** 14 (+2) **CON:** 12 (+1) **INT:** 14 (+2) **WIS:** 13 (+1) **CHA:** 12 (+1)

**Resistances:** cold, necrotic; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks  
**Vulnerabilities:** thunder  
**Condition Immunities:** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, grappled, prone, restrained  
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 11  
**Languages:** Dwarvish; understands Common but rarely responds

**Actions**

**Ledger Lash.** Ranged Spell Attack: +4 to hit, range 30 ft., one target. Hit: 7 (2d6) psychic damage, and the target hears overlapping names until the start of its next turn.

**Name Overload (Recharge 5–6).** One creature within 30 ft. must make a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, it takes 10 (3d6) psychic damage and has disadvantage on its next Intelligence or Wisdom check before the end of its next turn. On a success, it takes half damage and suffers no disadvantage.

**Death / Release Effect**

If violently reduced to 0 hit points, the survivor slate flashes and the Resonance Pulse Track advances by 1.

## Encounter 2 — The Door-Bracers

### Summary

Three ghostly dwarves stand at a pressure-seal lever, endlessly trying to brace and close the route behind the escapees.

They are caught at the moment of sealing. They do not hate the party; they are terrified of whatever was below and desperate to keep the door held.

### Default Behavior

The Door-Bracers repeat the same motion: shoulders into the lever, feet sliding on stone, hands overlapping on iron. Their effort never completes. The lever moves an inch, shudders, and resets.

### Interaction Options

- A character may help brace the lever physically.
- A character may study the mechanism and tell the spirits when to push.
- A character may speak a steady command in Dwarvish.
- An attuned character may sense the correct rhythm before the lever moves.

### Suggested Checks

- **Athletics DC 15:** Help brace or complete the motion.
- **Investigation DC 14:** Understand the lever's pressure-seal timing.
- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Give the correct command cadence.
- **Insight DC 13:** Realize the spirits are not trying to trap the party; they are trying to seal something below.
- **Mountain Attunement:** An attuned character may gain advantage on one timing-related check but feels pressure behind the eyes afterward.

### Success

The lever completes its motion for one breath. The spirits look relieved and reveal a safe structural clue, such as:

> "Do not pull against the mountain. Wait for the second pulse. Then move."

The party may gain advantage on one future check to time movement through a resonance pulse in Packet 6.

### If Attacked

The pressure mechanism slams violently. Treat the area as a short hazard before or during combat. Each creature within 10 ft. of the lever must make a **DC 14 Dexterity saving throw** or take 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage and be knocked prone.

### Simple Stat Block — Door-Bracer Spirit

Use one stat block for each of the three spirits, or treat the trio as a single swarm-like encounter.

**Medium undead, neutral**  
**AC:** 14  
**HP:** 18 each  
**Speed:** 20 ft.  
**STR:** 14 (+2) **DEX:** 10 (+0) **CON:** 14 (+2) **INT:** 10 (+0) **WIS:** 12 (+1) **CHA:** 10 (+0)

**Resistances:** cold, necrotic; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks  
**Vulnerabilities:** thunder  
**Condition Immunities:** exhaustion, frightened, grappled, prone, restrained  
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 11  
**Languages:** Dwarvish

**Actions**

**Spectral Hammer-Fist.** Melee Spell Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 6 (1d8 + 2) force damage.

**Brace Pulse.** Each hostile creature within 10 ft. must succeed on a **DC 13 Strength saving throw** or be pushed 10 ft. away from the lever and knocked prone. On a success, the creature is not pushed and remains standing.

**Reaction**

**Hold the Seal.** When a creature within 5 ft. of the spirit is hit by an attack, the spirit imposes disadvantage on the attack roll, appearing between the attacker and the target. It can use this reaction only while hostile.

**Death / Release Effect**

If all three spirits are violently destroyed, the pressure-seal slams open and shut once. The Resonance Pulse Track advances by 1.

## Encounter 3 — The Child Under the Bench

### Summary

A small dwarven child-spirit hides beneath a cracked stone bench in the Last Breath Refuge, clutching a slate tag that bears a family mark.

This spirit should not be a combat encounter by default. It is a fear-and-empathy test. The party can learn a small but important emotional truth: some evacuees were children, and some were waiting for adults who never arrived.

### Default Behavior

The child hides silently. The only signs are a small pale hand around a slate tag, soft breathing that is not real breath, and a whisper asking whether the sky is supposed to be this cold.

### Interaction Options

- A character may speak softly without approaching too quickly.
- A character may offer warmth, food, a light, or a promise not to send the child back down.
- A character may show the survivor slate or ask about the slate tag.
- A character may simply sit nearby and wait.

### Suggested Checks

- **Persuasion DC 13:** Coax the child out gently.
- **Insight DC 12:** Understand that direct questioning is frightening the child.
- **Religion DC 13:** Offer a simple rite of comfort without forcing release.
- **Survival DC 13:** Recognize signs that the child arrived cold, exhausted, and separated from family.

### Success

The child whispers one clue:

> "They said not to go back down. They said if the bells stopped, we had to stop listening."

The child then fades partly, but not fully. This should feel unresolved, not triumphant.

### If Attacked

The child does not become a normal combatant. Instead, adult protective spirits manifest defensively or the room itself reacts with a grief backlash.

If the party directly attacks the child, each attacking creature must make a **DC 14 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, it takes 10 (3d6) psychic damage and is frightened until the end of its next turn. On a success, it takes half damage and is not frightened.

Then two **Door-Bracer Spirits** or one **Protective Refuge Spirit** may manifest.

### Simple Stat Block — Child Under the Bench

**Small undead, neutral**  
**AC:** 12  
**HP:** 9  
**Speed:** 0 ft., fly 20 ft. (hover)  
**STR:** 3 (-4) **DEX:** 14 (+2) **CON:** 10 (+0) **INT:** 10 (+0) **WIS:** 12 (+1) **CHA:** 14 (+2)

**Resistances:** cold, necrotic; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks  
**Vulnerabilities:** thunder  
**Condition Immunities:** exhaustion, grappled, prone, restrained  
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 11  
**Languages:** Dwarvish; understands simple Common

**Actions**

The child does not attack unless the DM chooses to represent a grief reflex.

**Frightened Cry (Triggered Only).** Each hostile creature within 15 ft. must make a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, it takes 7 (2d6) psychic damage and cannot willingly move closer to the child until the end of its next turn. On a success, it takes half damage and can move normally.

**Protective Refuge Spirit**

If needed, use the Door-Bracer Spirit stat block but reduce HP to 16 and replace Brace Pulse with the following:

**Protect the Small.** One hostile creature within 10 ft. must make a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, it is frightened of the spirit until the end of its next turn.

**Death / Release Effect**

If the child is violently reduced to 0 hit points, the Resonance Pulse Track advances by 2 instead of 1. This should be framed as the mountain grieving harder, not as a reward for combat.

## Encounter 4 — The Sooted Prayer

### Summary

At the prayer niche, a dwarven spirit repeatedly smears soot across a queenly relief, whispering apologies.

The act should initially look suspicious or blasphemous, but it is not hatred. The spirit is trying to hide the queenly figure from grief, memory, or the king's attention.

### Default Behavior

The spirit kneels, reaches toward a cold ceremonial sconce, blackens its fingers with old soot, and smears the queenly face again and again. A blue seam cuts through the relief's crown.

### Interaction Options

- A character may observe before judging.
- A character may ask why the spirit is covering the face.
- A character may use Religion or Insight to recognize protective mourning behavior.
- A character may help shield the relief without naming the queen.

### Suggested Checks

- **Religion DC 14:** Understand that the act resembles protective mourning or warding, not desecration.
- **Insight DC 13:** The spirit is ashamed and afraid, not angry.
- **Investigation DC 14:** The soot was applied many times by many hands, not in one act of vandalism.
- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Understand fragments such as "do not say her name" and "he hears grief before words."

### Success

The spirit whispers:

> "Do not say her name in the stair. He hears grief before he hears words."

The party learns that the queenly figure mattered, that her image became emotionally dangerous, and that naming or mourning her may attract attention deeper inside Stillpeak. Do not reveal the queen's death yet.

### If Attacked

The blue seam across the relief flashes. The spirit rises, not furious but terrified, and tries to drive the party away from the image.

### Simple Stat Block — Sooted Prayer Spirit

**Medium undead, neutral**  
**AC:** 13  
**HP:** 26  
**Speed:** 0 ft., fly 25 ft. (hover)  
**STR:** 8 (-1) **DEX:** 13 (+1) **CON:** 12 (+1) **INT:** 11 (+0) **WIS:** 15 (+2) **CHA:** 14 (+2)

**Resistances:** cold, necrotic; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks  
**Vulnerabilities:** thunder  
**Condition Immunities:** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, grappled, prone, restrained  
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 12  
**Languages:** Dwarvish

**Actions**

**Sooted Touch.** Melee Spell Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 7 (2d6) necrotic damage, and the target's vision is smeared with dark afterimages until the end of its next turn. During that time, the target has disadvantage on its next Perception check relying on sight.

**Do Not Speak Her Name (Recharge 5–6).** Each hostile creature within 20 ft. that can hear the spirit must make a **DC 14 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, it takes 10 (3d6) psychic damage and cannot speak above a whisper until the end of its next turn. On a success, it takes half damage and can speak normally.

**Reaction**

**Cover the Face.** When a creature targets the queenly relief, names the queenly figure, or damages the niche, the spirit imposes disadvantage on that creature's next attack roll or ability check before the end of its turn.

**Death / Release Effect**

If violently reduced to 0 hit points, the soot vanishes from the relief for one round, exposing the queenly face. The Resonance Pulse Track advances by 1, and an attuned character hears a painful inner tone.

## DM Guidance — Using These Encounters Together

Use at most two of these encounters as full scenes in Packet 6 unless the party is strongly engaging with the spirits.

Recommended placement:

- **Last Breath Refuge:** Ledger Keeper and/or Child Under the Bench.
- **Pressure-seal area or threshold hardware:** Door-Bracers.
- **Broken Muster Niches / Prayer Niche:** Sooted Prayer.

The best Chapter 6 pattern is:

1. The party first sees a spirit as eerie but nonhostile.
2. Respectful interaction produces a clue or advantage.
3. An opportunistic evil creature later behaves differently, making the contrast clear.
4. The party learns that Stillpeak contains victims, parasites, and future true enemies — not one flat category of monsters.

---

## Balance Notes

These stat blocks are intentionally simple and modest for a level 7 party.

They are not meant to threaten the party in fair combat. Their job is to make reckless violence costly through resonance escalation, psychic backlash, positioning problems, and moral consequence.

If the party attacks every spirit, do not turn Chapter 6 into a long combat gauntlet. Use the Resonance Pulse Track, memory backlash, and environmental consequences to signal that violence is making the mountain worse.
