### Scene Purpose

Transition from the observatory’s controlled environment into afflicted wilderness.

Introduce environmental pressure gently: cold, silence, faint tremors.

Foreshadow the Karagvorn Line without punishing the party.

Let the Tremorscope ‘speak’ in small pulses—enough to build trust.

### Environmental Overview — The Cold Pass

A forested mountain trail descending from Southwatch into the lower eastern foothills. The route is old and known, but recent weeks have made it feel abandoned. The farther the party travels, the more the world seems to absorb motion and sound.

**Travel time:** 2–4 hours on foot (pacing dependent).

**Key traits:** dense evergreens, snow-sheathed branches, unnaturally muffled wind, sparse wildlife that fades to none.

**Tremorscope:** occasional faint pulses; lines appear thin and intermittent.

### Travel Structure (Recommended Beats)

Run as four beats. Each beat adds one new ‘wrongness’ without forcing combat.

#### Beat 1 — Leaving the Observatory Grounds

Ease the transition; introduce cold pressure as discomfort, not penalty.

- **Cold Exposure Check (CON DC 10):** On failure, describe numb fingers, stinging lungs; no mechanical penalty yet.
- **Survival (DC 12):** Wolf tracks heading south that abruptly turn back north, as if recoiling.
- **Tremorscope:** faint, irregular lines; one soft pulse like a distant knock.

### Mini-Beat: Phase 1 — The Disturbance (Immediate Aftermath)

A deer is perfectly frozen in the middle of the trail.

Upon touching the deer, it into powder:

The fine blue frost doesn’t settle naturally. Instead, it hangs—just for a moment too long—like breath suspended in the air.

Then it pulls. Not downward… but sideways.

**DM Notes:**
- This is subtle. No wind. No magic aura.
- The frost is being drawn somewhere.

**Check Opportunities:**
- **Perception (DC 12):** The particles drift toward the treeline, not with gravity.
- **Arcana (DC 13):** No spell effect—this is environmental, systemic.
- **Nature (DC 12):** Snow doesn’t behave like this. Ever.

This establishes: the force is active, not residual.

### Phase 2 — The Trail Corruption

Following the drift (or continuing forward naturally), the party notices:

The snow ahead loses its softness. It compresses into a glassy crust—translucent in patches, like thin ice forming over something that isn’t water.

Boots crunch differently now:
- Less snow
- More fracture

Then:

Beneath your feet—faint, irregular—something clicks.

Not a tremor yet.

More like:

pressure adjusting.

### Phase 3 — The First “False Tremor”

Give them a near-miss:

A soft vibration passes through the ground—quick, uneven. Not the slow, mournful pulse you felt before. This is sharper. Nervous.

**Important distinction:**
- Observatory pulses = deep, sorrowful, intentional
- This = shallow, reactive, wrong

Let them feel:

This is not the mountain itself…

This is something responding to it.

### Phase 4 — The Reveal Clue (Critical)

They find it before the encounter triggers:

Ahead, the trail bends around a cluster of low, ice-coated stones. Frost clings to them unnaturally thick—layered, like something has been breathing on them for hours.

Then:

The frost is not still.

Tiny cracks.

Expanding.

Contracting.

Like something underneath is:

trying to wake up.

### Phase 5 — The Tremorscope Confirms (Key Payoff)

If they check it (or you prompt it subtly):

The Tremorscope flickers—thin jagged lines scratching across the glass.

Not pointing forward.

Pointing… directly at the stones.

This is important:
- It validates the tool
- It localizes the threat
- It builds trust early (per canon)

### Phase 6 — The Wake-Up Trigger

Do NOT have the mephits just appear.

Trigger them through interaction or proximity.

**Options:**
- A player steps too close
- Someone touches the frost
- A loud sound (weapon, voice, spell)
- Or even just waiting too long

Then:

The ice fractures inward—like something pulling itself free from inside the frost.

A sharp, chittering crack echoes through the trees.

And the ground gives a sudden, violent shudder—short, erratic—nothing like the mountain’s pulse.

### Encounter Module Enhancement — Tremor-Burrow Ice Mephits (Revised)

This encounter should emphasize environmental instability over creature lethality. The ice mephits are not the primary threat—they are manifestations of localized disturbance caused by the mountain’s deeper pulse.

#### Encounter Structure (Wave-Based Emergence)

Begin with 2 Ice Mephits erupting from the ground. At the start of Round 2, a third mephit bursts from a newly fractured section of terrain. If the encounter resolves too quickly, an optional fourth mephit may emerge at the start of Round 3. This creates the impression that the ground itself is producing them, preventing immediate elimination through focused attacks.

#### Environmental Hazard — Tremor Pulse (Initiative Count 20)

At initiative count 20 (losing ties), the battlefield destabilizes:
- 2–3 random 10-ft squares fracture, becoming difficult terrain
- Creatures in those spaces must succeed on a **DC 12 Dexterity save** or fall prone
- One affected square becomes **Thin Ice**: on a failed save, the creature is restrained until it uses an action to free itself

This mechanic reinforces positioning and introduces early risk without overwhelming the party.

#### Enhanced Death Burst (Revised Trait)

When a mephit dies, its form shatters violently:
- Creatures within 5 ft. must make a **DC 11 Dexterity save**
- On failure, take **7 (2d6) cold damage**
- The space becomes a slick ice patch (difficult terrain) until the end of the encounter

This discourages clustering and adds consequence to melee engagement.

### Design Intent

This encounter teaches that terrain and tremors are the true adversaries. The mephits are reactive and unstable, reinforcing that the party is interacting with a disturbed environment—not fighting a contained enemy force.

### Encounter Read-Aloud Trigger

The Tremorscope vibrates sharply in your hand—stronger than anything you’ve felt since leaving Southwatch.

Thin, jagged lines etch themselves across its glass… faster… tighter… converging.

Then—one clean pulse.

It hits your bones a heartbeat before the sound reaches your ears.

The ground answers.

Snow lifts in a sudden ripple. The trail splits with a sharp crack as something forces its way upward from beneath the surface.

Three jagged shapes burst free—ice and wind coiling into shrieking forms, their wings snapping open as the cold itself seems to take shape before you.

### Environmental Rule (This Is Important)

#### Tremor Cracks (Initiative Count 20)

At initiative count 20 (losing ties):
- A random 10-ft square fractures
- Becomes difficult terrain
- Creatures in that space must succeed on a **DC 11 DEX save** or fall prone

This is doing heavy lifting:
- Teaches tremor mechanics early
- Adds chaos without increasing lethality
- Rewards positioning (fighter + paladin benefit)

### Encounter Behavior (Running It Well)

Do NOT play these like tactical monsters.

They are:
- Erratic
- Reactive
- Slightly panicked

#### Behavior Script

- **Round 1:** Burst from ground → scatter → breath attack if possible
- **Round 2:** Stay airborne, harass backline (warlock/ranger)
- **Round 3+:** One tries to flee into snow fissure if low HP

This keeps the fight:
- Short
- Dynamic
- Slightly unnatural

### Treasure

Do NOT over-reward this fight. Early bloat kills tension.

#### Frostglass Shards (3)

Translucent blue fragments from the mephits’ bodies.

**Use (Bonus Action):** Gain resistance to cold damage until end of next turn.

**Design Purpose:**
- Teaches resource timing
- Helps later cold mechanics
- Feels “of the mountain,” not magical loot

#### Tremor-Touched Stone (1)

A smooth, faintly vibrating stone uncovered by the tremor.

**Use (Action):** Place on ground → it slowly rolls toward nearest tremor source.  
Works once, then becomes inert.

**Design Purpose:**
- Soft foreshadow of Tremorscope
- Encourages players to think: “The mountain can be read”

#### Beat 2 — The Silent Pines

First clear sign the environment is being dampened.

- **Nature (DC 13):** This is not weather. Something is suppressing motion and dispersing energy.
- **Insight (DC 12):** A faint emotional drag—melancholy pressing down.
- **Tremorscope:** a brief spike, then silence.
- **Atmospheric cue:** a pinecone drops into snow with no sound.

#### Beat 3 — The Abandoned Hunter’s Camp

Evidence the hunter wasn’t alone, and the forest ‘removes’ people without struggle.

- **Investigation (DC 14):** Camp abandoned in panic; at least two sets of footprints; one vanishes into the woods.
- **Survival (DC 12):** No struggle marks; just disappearance.
- **Arcana (DC 12):** The air carries a faint ‘drag’; minor magic feels dulled; a spark delay in magical flame.

#### Beat 4 — The First Tremor

Connect the journey directly to Stillpeak’s heartbeat.

- **Perception (DC 12):** The ground pulses twice, slow and deliberate, felt in teeth and bones.
- **Arcana (DC 14):** The cadence feels intentional rather than tectonic.
- **Tremorscope:** etched lines pulse in sync with the tremor.

### Karagvorn Line Foreshadowing (Pre-Boundary Signs)

Items feel subtly heavier (a pound or two) when checked; metal develops faint frost even when held.

Small spells ‘work,’ but feel slightly delayed or less useful; compasses may drift or hesitate.

- **Arcana (DC 12):** Something is reinterpreting rules; not dispelling magic, but shifting assumptions.

### Optional Encounter — The Frost-Stilled Deer (Non-Combat)

A deer stands perfectly still. Snow covers its back, unmelted. Its eyes are frozen open. It is dead, but there is no sign of struggle. This is the party’s first close look at the curse’s ‘stillness’ effect.

- **Medicine (DC 12):** Dead, but not from cold exposure in a normal sense.
- **Nature (DC 13):** This is unnatural preservation.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** Faint grief resonance; the body collapses into powder-fine frost if touched too long.

### Arrival Transition to Cinderwatch

Lanterns appear through the snow. Cinderwatch looks lifeless—no barking dogs, no animal sounds, no laughter. The Tremorscope pulses softly, like something far above is trying to be heard.
