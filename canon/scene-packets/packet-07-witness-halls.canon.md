## 2. Narrative Setup

The Witness Halls sit along Bridgeward Way before the Queen's Bridge. They were not built as a defensive dungeon. They were formal dwarven civic/religious approach chambers: places where processions gathered, witnesses waited, offerings were prepared, and the bridge was approached with ceremony.

Now they are empty except for dead lamp infrastructure, covered queenly imagery, memory-bound spirits, and cursed crystal growths that respond to grief and attention.

The party should understand by the end of this area:

- The bridge ahead was sacred or civically important.
- The queen was loved and central to the old kingdom's religious/civic life.
- Her memory became dangerous after the tragedy.
- Spirits are gathering more densely near the bridge.
- Grief-crystal growths are becoming violent and autonomous.

Do not reveal the full queen's fall here. The Witness Halls prepare the revelation; the Queen's Bridge delivers it.

## 3. Read-Aloud Scene Introduction

Use as the party leaves the Empty City Verge and enters the formal halls before the bridge.

> The route from the overlook descends into a broad interior hall.
>
> The stone here is not emergency-cut. It is deliberate. Formal. Built for people to walk slowly while others watched.
>
> Dead lantern cups line the walls at even intervals. Above them, hooks still hold scraps of dust-rotted fabric that might once have been banners.
>
> The crystal seams are brighter here, threading through the floor in pale lines that do not respect the old dwarven patterns.
>
> Ahead, the passage divides into chambers of waiting, prayer, and witness.
>
> The air feels full of people who are not here.

## 4. Environment Overview

### Visual Motifs

- Processional floor grooves and wide walking lanes.
- Dead lantern infrastructure placed for ceremony and visibility.
- Dust-rotted banners.
- Covered or unfinished queenly reliefs.
- Viewing niches facing toward the bridge route.
- Small spirit loops: bowing, waiting, carrying lanterns, holding cloth, lowering eyes.
- Crystal growths increasingly intruding through formal stonework.

### Spirit Behavior

Witness Hall spirits are not interactive NPCs by default. They are partial witnesses and attendants caught in pre-tragedy or immediate pre-procession loops.

They may:

- repeat a gesture
- whisper a fragment
- look toward the bridge
- avoid looking at queenly faces
- react if the party speaks too loudly about the queen

### Resonance Pressure

If the party loudly damages queen imagery, uses thunder recklessly, mocks spirits, or speaks the queen's name if one is later established, advance the local resonance pressure or trigger the Animus early.

## 5. Running the Location

The Witness Halls should be compact. Do not require every area to be fully solved. The area works best if the party samples 2–4 clues, has one minor spirit/social moment, and then faces the Animus before the final arch.

Recommended order:

1. Processional Entry.
2. Hall of Witness Niches.
3. Gallery of Covered Faces.
4. Crystal Growth Chamber / Animus encounter.
5. Last Arch Before the Span.

---

## 6. Keyed Areas

### Area 1 — Processional Entry

**Purpose:** Establish that Bridgeward was ceremonial/civic, not merely utilitarian.

**Read-Aloud:**

> The first hall is wide enough for six dwarves to walk abreast.
>
> Its floor is carved with shallow parallel grooves, not for drainage or carts, but for procession lines. Along the walls, empty lantern brackets face inward, angled to light those who passed through the center.
>
> A few scraps of pale fabric cling to banner hooks, too old to show color.
>
> The passage does not say hurry.
>
> It says be seen.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **History DC 14:** This was a processional route, not a military corridor.
- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Read worn inscriptions referencing witness, passage, crown, and span.
- **Religion DC 14:** The hall's layout supports sacred movement or vow procession.
- **Investigation DC 13:** Dead lamps were arranged to illuminate people in the center of the hall.

**Success:** The party understands the bridge ahead had public ceremonial importance.

**Failure:** They still see formal construction, but the meaning becomes clearer in later areas.

**Spoken Discovery Cue:**

> Whoever walked here was meant to be watched.
>
> Not judged.
>
> Honored.

---

### Area 2 — Hall of Witness Niches

**Purpose:** Minor spirit/social scene. Show spirits gathering closer to the bridge and establish witness roles.

**Environment:** Small niches line both sides of the hall. Some contain stone benches. Others contain dead lantern stands, prayer rests, or shallow carved name panels. Pale spirit-shapes repeat small gestures: lowering heads, lifting lanterns, touching two fingers to brow and heart.

**Read-Aloud:**

> The side walls open into shallow niches.
>
> At first they look empty.
>
> Then one fills with a pale dwarf holding an unlit lantern. Another with a woman pressing two fingers to her brow. Another with a child standing too still beside an adult's knee.
>
> They do not speak to you.
>
> They look past you, toward the bridge that is not yet visible.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Insight DC 13:** The spirits are not guarding the route; they are waiting to witness something.
- **Religion DC 14:** Their gestures are reverent, formal, and pre-tragedy, not funerary yet.
- **Perception DC 13:** Notice several spirits avoid looking at any queenly symbol.
- **Persuasion DC 14:** A gentle, respectful address may coax one brief fragment.

**Success:** The party learns these spirits are witnesses bound to pre-fall roles.

**Failure:** The spirits remain loops, but do not become hostile.

**Optional Spoken Fragments:**

Use one or two.

> "Lanterns high when she passes."

> "Eyes lowered until the crown reaches the span."

> "No shouting in the witness hall. The bridge carries sound."

> "She walks for all houses today. Even the lower ones."

**DM Note:** This scene should be brief. It is not a full negotiation.

---

### Area 3 — Gallery of Covered Faces

**Purpose:** Show that the queen was loved and later became dangerous to remember directly.

**Environment:** Reliefs along the walls show a queenly figure in processional context. Some faces are covered in soot. Some are veiled with ancient cloth. Some were chiseled away carefully. Some were left unfinished.

**Read-Aloud:**

> The next chamber is lined with reliefs.
>
> The same figure appears again and again: a crowned woman beside a bridge, a mountain arch, a lantern procession, a gathered city.
>
> Or almost appears.
>
> Here, soot covers her face. There, a veil of brittle cloth hangs from tiny stone hooks. Farther on, the sculptor stopped before carving her eyes.
>
> The damage does not feel like hatred.
>
> It feels like people trying to survive memory.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Insight DC 13:** The covering/damage is protective, not hateful.
- **Religion DC 14:** The soot and veils resemble mourning wards and restraint gestures.
- **Investigation DC 15:** Some reliefs were altered after completion; others were abandoned mid-work after the tragedy.
- **History DC 14:** The queen had public civic/religious significance.
- **Perception DC 13:** Spirits in nearby niches avert their eyes from uncovered or unfinished faces.

**Success:** The party understands her memory was loved but dangerous.

**Failure:** The party may initially misread the damage as vandalism; later bridge scenes should correct this.

**Spoken Discovery Cue:**

> These were not defaced by enemies.
>
> They were covered by mourners who were afraid mourning would be heard.

**Optional Attunement Cue:**

For Erny or Maelreth:

> The covered faces make the pressure behind your eyes ease.
>
> The uncovered ones make it sharper.

---

### Area 4 — Crystal Growth Chamber

**Purpose:** Major combat encounter with the Witness-Hall Grief-Crystal Animus.

**Environment:** A formal chamber has been overrun by a large grief-crystal formation. Dead braziers and lamp stands show the room was once used for preparation or procession staging. The crystal has grown through a central relief, floor grooves, and witness benches.

**Read-Aloud Before Activation:**

> The air changes before the room opens.
>
> You feel it in your teeth.
>
> The chamber ahead was once formal: benches along the walls, a central lane, dead braziers placed to light the passage forward.
>
> But the far wall is no longer only wall.
>
> A mass of crystal has grown through it in jagged layers: rose-white, cold blue, green-white, yellow like old lamplight trapped in stone.
>
> The formation pulses slowly.
>
> Not with light.
>
> With pressure.

**Trigger Options:**

- The party approaches within 20 feet.
- Erny or Maelreth studies the formation through attunement.
- The party damages the crystal.
- The party speaks openly about the queen in this chamber.
- The DM needs forward pressure.

**Activation Read-Aloud:**

> The crystal seam does not crack.
>
> It unfolds.
>
> A shape pulls itself out of the wall in grinding angles: shoulders of rose-white stone, arms of jagged blue mineral, a headless mass of pulsing crystal where a heart should be.
>
> The floor trembles under it.
>
> Not footsteps.
>
> Aftershocks.
>
> Erny and Maelreth, you feel it before it turns. A pressure behind the eyes. A pulse in the teeth. A grief that is not asking for mercy, but still somehow recognizes you.
>
> Then it comes for you.

**Encounter:** Use **Witness-Hall Grief-Crystal Animus** from `packet-07-monsters.canon.md`.

**Terrain Features:**

- **Crystal-Split Floor:** Several 10-foot patches are difficult terrain.
- **Dead Braziers:** Can be overturned or relit. A relit brazier may reveal safe footing, granting advantage once on an Acrobatics or Perception check.
- **Processional Groove:** A character who follows the old central lane can move normally through one difficult terrain patch.
- **Resonant Wall:** Thunder damage against the wall causes a pulse; each creature within 10 feet makes **DC 14 Strength save** or is pushed 10 feet.

**Additional Checks During Combat:**

- **Arcana DC 15:** Understand the creature is grief-resonance growth, not commanded intelligence.
- **Religion DC 14:** Recognize the emotional pressure as corrupted mourning, not an evil spirit.
- **Perception DC 13:** Notice the processional grooves provide safer movement.
- **Athletics or Acrobatics DC 14:** Cross fractured terrain without speed loss.

**Success / Defeat Cue:**

> The crystal body breaks apart in pieces too heavy to fall naturally.
>
> For a moment, each shard glows with the same dim pulse.
>
> Then the light leaves it.
>
> Erny and Maelreth, the pressure behind your eyes vanishes so suddenly it almost feels like loss.

**Post-Encounter Discovery:**

A cleared line of sight reveals the route to the final arch before the bridge.

---

### Area 5 — Last Arch Before the Span

**Purpose:** Give the party one breath before the Queen's Bridge and preview the reenactment.

**Read-Aloud:**

> Beyond the broken crystal chamber, the hall narrows.
>
> The dead lamps become more frequent. So do the spirits.
>
> Some stand with lanterns held high. Some kneel. Some hold strips of pale cloth. Some stare forward with faces that have not yet learned panic.
>
> At the end of the passage waits an arch wide enough for a procession.
>
> Beyond it, the mountain opens into impossible depth.
>
> The bridge is there.
>
> And on it, the dead are already moving.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Perception DC 13:** See spirit-shapes gathered on the bridge.
- **Survival DC 14:** Identify cracked sections and crossing risks.
- **Arcana or Religion DC 15:** The bridge is the strongest grief-resonance point encountered so far.
- **Insight DC 13:** The spirits are not waiting for the party; they are reliving a moment.

**Success:** The party may enter the bridge scene with one useful observation. Grant advantage on the first relevant Queen's Bridge check.

**Failure:** They enter without preparation, but no penalty beyond narrative uncertainty.
