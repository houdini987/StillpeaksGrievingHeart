# Scene Packet 8 Patch — Ending Style and King's Relic

## Canon Status

This patch is canon for Scene Packet 8 / Chapter 8: Throneward Descent.

It supersedes any Packet 8 read-aloud cue that uses overly elevated, AI-prose phrasing for the King's death or final release, including phrasing such as "Not as stone. Not as thunder." Keep the final spoken word clear, direct, sad, and table-natural.

## Spoken-Word Style Correction

Final read-aloud should sound like a DM speaking to people at the table, not like formal fantasy verse.

Prioritize:

- plain emotional language
- short but natural sentences
- clear cause and effect
- grief without melodrama
- satisfaction without victory-cheering
- closure for the King and Queen

Avoid:

- stacked poetic fragments
- abstract declarations
- repeated "not as / not as" constructions
- language that makes the ending feel generated or over-written
- queen dialogue, reunion speeches, or resurrection framing

The ending may be mythic in consequence, but the words at the table should be human.

## Updated Ending Q2 Decision

The release after the King's death or successful relic release is **layered but restrained**:

1. A sad silence falls over the room.
2. The green light drains from the chamber.
3. The mountain stops pressing against the party.
4. The old spirit loops across Stillpeak begin to end.
5. The Queen appears briefly as a quiet memory or silhouette.
6. The emotional read should be: the King and Queen are finally together, but no one in the room mistakes this for undoing what happened.

This is not a resurrection. It is not a long conversation. It is the end of a grief that was never allowed to finish.

## Mercy Death Read-Aloud Replacement

Use after the party grants mercy during the clarity window.

> The King goes still.
>
> For a moment, nothing else happens.
>
> Then the room gets quiet in a way it has not been quiet before.
>
> The throne cracks under him. The green light drains out of the crystal veins, first around the throne, then across the floor, then up the walls.
>
> The pressure in your skulls eases. The air feels heavy, but it is only air now.
>
> Around the chamber, the spirits lower their heads. No one cheers. No one speaks.
>
> One by one, the old sounds stop: the running footsteps, the counting, the prayers, the same last moments repeating over and over.
>
> Behind the broken throne, a soft shape appears in the fading green light. A dwarven queen, seen only for a breath.
>
> The King looks peaceful now.
>
> Not happy. Not forgiven of everything.
>
> But finished.
>
> And for the first time, you get the sense that wherever he is now, he is no longer alone.
>
> Then the Queen's shape fades, the last green light goes out, and Stillpeak is silent.

## Successful Relic Release Read-Aloud Replacement

Use if the party succeeds on the narrow relic release path instead of immediately killing the King.

> The relic steadies in the green light.
>
> The throne pulls against it for one hard breath.
>
> Then something gives.
>
> The crystal fused through the King's crown and hands dulls from green to gray. He slumps forward, breathing like a man who has just come up from deep water.
>
> The room gets quiet in a way it has not been quiet before.
>
> Around the chamber, the spirits lower their heads. No one cheers. No one speaks.
>
> The old sounds begin to stop: the running footsteps, the counting, the prayers, the same last moments repeating over and over.
>
> Behind the broken throne, a soft shape appears in the fading green light. A dwarven queen, seen only for a breath.
>
> The King sees her.
>
> He does not reach for her. He does not ask her to stay.
>
> He only closes his eyes.
>
> Whether he dies in that moment or a few breaths later, he dies as himself.
>
> And when the Queen's shape fades, the mountain lets go.

## The King's Pendant — Mythic Loot Drop

After the King dies or is fully released, the party may recover the pendant from around his neck.

### Name

**The First Green Heart**

Alternate names the DM may use at the table:

- **The King's Green Heart**
- **The Heart-Seed of Stillpeak**
- **The First Crystal**

### Description

A deep green crystal pendant hangs on a blackened royal chain around the King's neck. It is not large — roughly thumb-sized — but it feels heavier than metal when held.

Inside the crystal, green light turns slowly, like something breathing under glass.

This is the original crystal source that began the chain of events before the Queen's death: the first great Abbathor-touched reservoir of power that Dhurak, Veyra, and the King failed to understand, failed to fear, and eventually used to consume the mountain.

It is the concentrated remnant of the same energy source that once swallowed Stillpeak.

### Canon Role

The First Green Heart is a mythic-level relic of unimaginable power.

It should feel beyond normal 5e treasure. The campaign is ending with this party, so the item may be wondrous, dangerous, and oversized in implication without needing long-term balance.

It is not ordinary loot. It is a campaign-ending artifact.

### Suggested Properties

Use only if the party asks what it does or if the DM wants an epilogue-facing item card.

- **World-Scale Reservoir:** The crystal contains enough dormant resonance to power or destroy a city-scale magical work if awakened by someone who understands it.
- **Mountain Memory:** While held, the bearer can feel the outline of Stillpeak's grief, old tunnels, dead halls, and sealed wounds as emotional impressions rather than a map.
- **Unspendable Safely:** Any attempt to use it for raw power risks repeating the original sin of Stillpeak: preservation, greed, and certainty masquerading as necessity.
- **One Safe Use:** It may be laid to rest, buried, shattered in a sacred place, given to a trusted divine/natural order, or sealed where no king, druid, priest, or miner can reach it easily.
- **Temptation:** A character holding it understands, for one terrible second, why the Druids thought they could fix death.

### Mechanical Guidance

Do not turn this into a standard combat item unless the campaign unexpectedly continues.

If mechanics are needed for a final epilogue or one last dramatic act:

- Once, the bearer may invoke the First Green Heart to stabilize a collapsing magical structure, suppress a mountain-scale resonance pulse, or seal a catastrophic magical wound.
- Doing so requires a group skill challenge rather than a single roll.
- Suggested challenge: 5 successes before 3 failures using Arcana, Religion, Nature, Persuasion, or relevant tool/lore checks, DC 18.
- Failure should not instantly kill the party, but it should make clear that no mortal person should treat the relic as a tool.

### DM Warning

The First Green Heart is fun because this party's campaign ends here. If the party ever continues, this item should become an epilogue burden, divine charge, sealed artifact, or future campaign problem — not a repeatable player power source.

## Aftermath Inspection Cue

If the players inspect the King's body after the release:

> The crown and throne are ruined, but the pendant around his neck is still intact.
>
> It is a small green crystal on a blackened chain.
>
> After everything you have seen in this mountain, you know immediately that it is not just jewelry.
>
> This is where it started.
>
> Not the grief. Not the love. Not the Queen's fall.
>
> The power.
>
> The thing they found and thought they could control.

## Emotional Target

The final feeling should be sad satisfaction.

The party should feel that:

- the King made terrible choices
- the Druids' corruption weaponized his grief
- the Queen was never truly recoverable
- mercy was the right answer
- Stillpeak is still ruined, but no longer trapped
- the King and Queen are finally together beyond the mountain's reach
