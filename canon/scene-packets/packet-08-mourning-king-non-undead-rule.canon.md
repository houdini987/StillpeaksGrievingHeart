# Scene Packet 8 Supplement — Mourning King Non-Undead Rule

## Canon Status

This supplement is canon for Scene Packet 8 / Chapter 8 and overrides any shorthand that describes the Mourning King as a normal lich or undead creature.

## Core Rule

The Mourning King is **not truly undead**.

Use the lich chassis only as a Roll20 convenience if a chassis is needed for durability or saving throws, but do not import lich identity or combat assumptions. Canonically, he is a living-preserved monarch: throne-bound, crystal-sustained, and held far beyond a natural life by the green beam engine.

## Divine Sense / Evil Detection Boundary

Erny has repeatedly checked throughout the adventure for signs of evil, and the module should stay consistent with that theme.

The Mourning King should not register as undead, fiend, celestial, or straightforward evil. If Erny or another character uses divine sense-style detection near him, the correct read is:

- overwhelming grief
- ancient royal will
- crystal preservation
- oath-pressure
- refusal to release
- wrongness, but not a clean evil aura

This keeps Stillpeak's horror centered on grief, greed, denial, corruption, and preservation past mercy rather than a simple undead infestation.

## Druid Titan Boundary

Dhurak and Veyra are also not undead or fiends. Their corruption is Abbathor-touched crystal greed and centuries of warped Stone Druid power.

## Mechanical Consequence

Do not blindly apply undead-specific lich assumptions unless the DM chooses to for Roll20 convenience.

Avoid:

- phylactery language
- necromantic soul-devouring
- undead hunger
- simple evil lich framing
- mobile lich-duelist behavior
- the King commanding the Druids as minions
- the King acting as a normal spellcasting boss during combat

Prefer:

- throne-engine preservation
- crystal stasis
- grief-resonance command as ambient pressure, not tactical command
- royal authority reduced to a trapped echo
- living mercy denied
- prisoner-king bound into his own preservation machine
- the King as vessel/battery for the Druids' corrupted work

## Vessel / Throne Imprisonment Rule

The Mourning King is fastened to the throne and held prisoner by the throne-engine itself.

He is not the tactical commander of the final fight. He is the living vessel, battery, and grief-focus that Dhurak and Veyra are using and protecting.

He is not standing, dueling, pursuing, dodging, casting freely, or physically defending himself. The throne, crystal growths, and green beams hold him in place. His voice may emerge through the engine, but he is not directing the Druid titans.

While at least one green beam remains active:

- The King is restrained/throne-bound and cannot move.
- The King is effectively inert as a combatant.
- He takes no normal combat turn by default.
- He has no legendary actions.
- He has no legendary resistances.
- He does not cast normal spells from a spell list.
- He is not in command of Dhurak or Veyra.
- Dhurak and Veyra are actively protecting him because he is the vessel that sustains their preservation engine.
- Any magical pressure around him comes from the throne-engine, green beams, arena, and Druid titans, not from the King choosing combat actions.

When both beams break:

- The crystals binding him fracture.
- The throne-engine loses its hold.
- The King reaches a clarity/release window.
- He is no longer meaningfully protected by the Druid engine.
- The encounter should pivot toward resolution, mercy, or the narrow relic-salvation possibility rather than becoming a second full caster phase.

## Speech Boundary

The King **can speak**, but his speech should not imply tactical command over the Druids.

While bound, his speech should be fragmented, grief-struck, and partially involuntary:

- pleas
- denial
- memories of the queen
- flashes of clarity
- royal phrases emptied of command
- moments where the throne seems to speak through him

The King should not bark orders, coordinate Druid tactics, or act like the mastermind of the encounter. If a command-like phrase emerges from him, frame it as the throne-engine using old royal language, not the King exercising free will.

## Spellcasting Boundary

The King should **not** use a normal lich, wizard, or monarch spell list while bound.

If a magical effect appears to originate from him, describe it as engine-mediated rather than chosen spellcasting:

- the throne answers his grief
- the crystal seams flare when he speaks
- the beams translate refusal into pressure
- the mountain remembers through him
- Dhurak or Veyra channels power through the vessel

Avoid copied spell blocks for the King during the active Druid phase. Let the Druid titans, beam mechanics, arena pressure, and attunement backlash carry the combat.

## Legendary Resistance Rule

The Mourning King has **0 legendary resistances**.

Do not import the default lich legendary resistance trait.

His true defenses are:

- green beam protection from Dhurak and Veyra
- the throne-engine
- Druid interception and sacrifice
- the geode arena pressure
- the action economy created by two Druid titan beam anchors

This choice supports the theme that he is not a stock lich or evil undead boss. Once the beams are broken, the party's choices and control effects should be allowed to matter.

## Legendary Action Rule

The Mourning King has **0 legendary actions** while bound.

Do not use the default lich legendary action suite.

Any previously considered grief/crystal legendary effects should be reassigned to the throne-engine, arena, or Druid titan mechanics if still needed. The King himself should remain inert as a combatant until the beams break.

## Normal Turn Guidance

While the beams remain active, the King normally does not take a combat turn.

Recommended default:

- At the top of the round or on initiative 20, the DM may let him speak one short line, memory-fragment, or plea.
- This speech should not be an action, spell, or tactical command.
- Do not use high-level lich spells, Power Word effects, Disintegrate-style punishment, or mobile boss tactics.

After both beams break, his normal turn should not become a full offensive phase. The drama should move toward clarity, mercy, and resolution.

## Druid Sacrifice / Interception Rule

Dhurak and Veyra are trying to protect the King more than themselves.

If a party member attacks the Mourning King while one or both Druid beams remain active, a living Druid titan may sacrifice itself or interpose its beam-body to protect him.

Use this as the default rule:

- If both Druids are alive, the Druid whose beam is most visually connected to the incoming attack intercepts, or the DM chooses the better story fit.
- If only one Druid is alive, that Druid intercepts.
- The attack or effect is redirected to the intercepting Druid, or the Druid takes the damage/effect in the King's place.
- The interception should be visually obvious: green beam yanks taut, crystal roots lunge across the throne, a fault-wall erupts, or the Druid titan tears itself into the attack path.
- This does not require the Druid's normal reaction unless the DM wants a stricter tactical version. The purpose is to teach that the Druids must be dealt with first.

Optional stricter version:

- The intercepting Druid uses its reaction.
- If it has no reaction, the King instead receives the normal beam protection but not full interception.

When a Druid intercepts, describe self-sacrifice rather than self-preservation. They are preserving the vessel, not merely defending an ally.

Once both Druids are destroyed and both beams break, this interception ends.
