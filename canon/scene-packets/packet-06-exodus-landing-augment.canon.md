# Scene Packet 6 Supplement — Exodus Landing Augment

## Canon Status

This file is a canon scene supplement for `canon/scene-packets/packet-06-the-exodus-stair.canon.md`.

It augments **BEAT 6 — The Exodus Landing** only. It does not alter the already-played earlier Chapter 6 beats: summit door, Last Breath Refuge, Broken Muster Niches, Chain-Lift Void, Ice Mephit Squatters, Chain-Lift Crawler, or the First Echo of the Exodus.

Use this supplement when the party reaches Exodus Landing after the Chain-Lift Void and the table goal is to quickly orient the party toward Scene Packet 7 / Bridgeward Way.

When used as the first step into Packet 7, this room also functions as the physical setup for **Bridgeward Threshold**.

---

## Design Intent

**Runtime Target:** 10–15 minutes.

**Primary Purpose:** Orientation. The room should establish the party's available route options while making clear that only **Bridgeward Way** currently pulls them closer to the campaign goal.

**Interaction Mode:** Brief memory-echo, not a full spirit conversation and not a combat encounter.

**Spirit Escalation Rule:** The closer the party gets to the bridge where the queen fell, the more spirits should appear. In Exodus Landing, this begins as a gathering effect: more exodus spirits are visible near Bridgeward Way than elsewhere, but they remain memory-bound and noninteractive unless the DM chooses otherwise.

**Guaranteed Discovery:** Soot-Black Prayer Bead / mourning clue.

**Deep-Lore Boundary:** Do not reveal the queen's death, the immortal king truth, the exact bridge fall, or the final grief-source solution in this scene.

---

## Canon Map Identity and Orientation

**Official scene name:** Bridgeward Threshold when used as the opening beat of Packet 7.

**Map identity:** Exodus Landing / Four Ways.

The in-game Exodus Landing map is canon for this scene. Treat it as a broad, damaged dwarven sorting chamber: a square stone hall with four exits, a central raised route marker or civic plinth, ruined crowd-control rails, dead lamp infrastructure, debris, cracked paving, and wrong-colored cursed crystal seams in the walls.

The chamber is not a throne hall, shrine, guardroom, or treasure chamber. It is civic evacuation infrastructure: a controlled node where fleeing dwarves were counted, sorted, redirected, or held before routes were closed.

Use this map orientation:

| Map Direction | Canon Route | Visual Role |
|---|---|---|
| **North / top corridor** | Route back toward the Chain-Lift Void / Summit Egress | The party's arrival route from Packet 6. Narrower, damaged, descent-linked behind them. |
| **South / bottom stone ladder / lower passage** | **Bridgeward Way** | The formal forward route into Packet 7, depicted by the stone ladder / descent to a lower level. This is the Tremorscope-aligned route. |
| **West / left sealed side door** | Lower Holds | Blocked, braced, or sealed from this side. Evacuation closure logic. |
| **East / right reinforced side passage** | Royal Descent | Warning-marked, pressure-barred, emotionally oppressive, important but not the current route. |

The central raised stone feature is an old dwarven **route-control plinth** or **evacuation tally marker**, not an altar. It should read as practical civic infrastructure used by survivors and stewards under pressure.

The room's emotional logic is: **they made it this far, then had to choose who went where.**

## BEAT 6 Augment — The Landing of Four Ways

### Augment 1 — Regroup at the Landing

As the party finishes the Chain-Lift Void descent, resolve the physical regroup quickly.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Athletics, Acrobatics, or Survival DC 13** to descend/regroup cleanly.
- Grant advantage if the party uses rope, a climber's kit, the central chain, the Lift-Brake Pin, the Unbroken Anchor Ring, or coordinated help.

**Success:** The party reaches Exodus Landing together and in control.

**Failure:** One character arrives hard: 1d6 bludgeoning damage, dropped minor gear, or arrives prone/off-balance. Do not stall the scene.

**Spoken / Read-Aloud:**

> The shaft finally gives you floor.
>
> Not safety. Not comfort.
>
> Just space.
>
> After the narrow stair, the broken chain, and the vertical dark, this chamber feels almost impossible in its width. The mountain opens around you, and for the first time since entering Stillpeak, all of you can stand without feeling the walls press against your shoulders.

---

### Augment 2 — The Four Routes

The party sees the room's purpose quickly: this was an evacuation sorting point. Multiple routes exist, but only one route feels alive with the current story.

**Visible Routes:**

- **Summit Egress / Chain-Lift Return** — north/top corridor; the way the party came from.
- **Lower Holds** — west/left sealed doors, evacuation closure marks.
- **Royal Descent** — east/right reinforced side passage, cracked crest, warning marks, oppressive resonance.
- **Bridgeward Way** — south/bottom stone ladder or lower passage; darker descent, dust-shifted, aligned to the Tremorscope.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Dwarvish DC 12** or **Investigation DC 13** to read the route markers and understand the landing was built to sort evacuees by destination.

**Success:** The party understands this was a controlled evacuation node, not a casual hallway intersection.

**Failure:** The party can still identify the routes, but not the civic logic behind them.

**Spoken / Read-Aloud:**

> Low stone rails divide the floor into lanes. Not defensive barricades. Not market queues. Crowd-control.
>
> Bronze tally plates hang crooked from the walls. Dead lantern cups sit beside each route, each burned down to black residue.
>
> The arrows cut into the stone are old but readable. One points back toward the Summit Egress. One toward doors marked Lower Holds. One descends beneath a cracked royal crest.
>
> And one, half-hidden by dust and fallen stone, points toward a darker lower passage marked in old Dwarvish:
>
> **Bridgeward.**

### Augment 3 — Multiple Options, One True Pull

Use this section to let the party feel they have multiple options without letting Exodus Landing become a hub crawl.

#### Lower Holds

**Visual:** Heavy doors sealed from this side. Old pressure bars. Worn handprints.

**Meaning:** People passed through or were blocked behind these doors during the evacuation.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Perception DC 13** or **Investigation DC 14** to notice the seal was closed from the Landing side after movement through the area.

**Spoken Result:**

> The Lower Holds doors are not merely shut. They were sealed with intention.
>
> The pressure bars face this side. The handprints cluster around the locking points. Whoever closed these doors did it while standing where you stand now.

#### Royal Descent

**Visual:** Cracked royal crest, old warning marks, mourning-rite structure.

**Meaning:** Important, dangerous, emotionally loaded, but not the current path.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Religion DC 14** or **History DC 14** to understand the marks are not ordinary "keep out" warnings; they carry mourning and prohibition language.

**Spoken Result:**

> The Royal Descent does not feel abandoned.
>
> It feels refused.
>
> The marks around it are not simple warnings. Some are shaped like rites. Some like apologies. Some like orders given by people who did not want to obey them.

#### Bridgeward Way

**Visual:** The south/bottom stone ladder or lower passage descends away from the chamber. Dust is disturbed by no wind. Seam-glow faintly answers. The Tremorscope aligns here.

**Meaning:** This is the route toward the current goal and Scene Packet 7.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Arcana DC 15**, **Survival DC 14**, or **Tremorscope interpretation DC 13** to confirm the strongest resonance leads Bridgeward.

**Spoken Result:**

> The Tremorscope does not spin.
>
> It does not hesitate.
>
> It leans toward Bridgeward with the quiet certainty of a compass finding north. The mineral seams along that lower passage answer in a faint sequence, one pale pulse after another, like lights waking far beneath the stone.

### Augment 4 — First Gathering of Spirits

Use this as a brief memory-echo, not a conversation.

**Trigger:** When the party approaches, studies, or focuses on Bridgeward Way.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Insight DC 13**, **Religion DC 14**, or **Perception DC 14**.

**Success:** The character understands these spirits are not blocking the party; they are flowing toward the same direction, caught in old movement.

**Failure:** The spirits still appear, but the party may misread them as a threat.

**Spoken / Read-Aloud:**

> At first, you think the dust is moving.
>
> Then you realize the dust is passing through feet.
>
> One figure appears near the rails. Then another near the tally boards. Then three more at the mouth of Bridgeward Way. Dwarven shapes, pale as breath on cold glass, all moving in the same direction.
>
> None of them look at you.
>
> A woman clutching a broken lantern. A man with a child's slate tag tied around his wrist. Two armored attendants carrying nothing, though their hands are shaped as if bearing a stretcher.
>
> They are not marching.
>
> They are remembering how to flee.

**Optional Whisper Fragments:**

Use one to three, not all, unless the table slows down to listen.

> "Bridgeward next."
>
> "No names."
>
> "No crying when the chains hear."
>
> "Keep her covered."
>
> "Do not let the king hear grief before stone."

**Boundary:** Do not reveal queen death. Do not say this is where she fell. Only imply that Bridgeward grows more spiritually crowded and emotionally dangerous.

---

### Augment 5 — Soot-Black Prayer Bead Discovery

Place this near the Bridgeward route marker or beneath a dead signal-lamp station, not hidden in a loot pile.

**Suggested Check:**

- **Religion DC 14** or **Insight DC 13** to understand the bead as a mourning object, not a magic trinket.
- **Investigation DC 13** to find it if no one inspects the Bridgeward marker.

**Discovery Text:**

> Near the Bridgeward arrow, tucked beneath the lip of a dead signal-lamp basin, is a single blackened bead.
>
> It is smooth from handling. Not polished by craft, but by worry. By thumb. By repetition.
>
> When lifted, it is warm for one breath.
>
> Then cold.

**Mechanical Use:**

**Soot-Black Prayer Bead**

Once, while holding the bead during a memory echo, fear effect, grief-resonance effect, or mourning-related spiritual encounter, a character may gain advantage on one **Wisdom saving throw**, **Religion check**, or **Insight check**.

**Boundary:** The bead does not reveal the queen's death, protect directly from the king, or solve a major Packet 7 hazard. It helps the party endure or interpret grief.

**Spoken Result on Successful Religion / Insight:**

> This was not worn as decoration.
>
> Someone held this while trying not to speak.
>
> Not because silence was respectful. Because speaking grief aloud had become dangerous.

## Exit Button into Scene Packet 7

Use this once the party has oriented, seen the spirits, and found or noticed the bead.

**Spoken / Read-Aloud:**

> The spirits do not remain in the Landing.
>
> One by one, they pass into Bridgeward Way. Not walking exactly. Not vanishing exactly. More like the mountain is remembering them farther down.
>
> The Lower Holds remain sealed.
>
> The Royal Descent waits beneath its cracked crest.
>
> The Summit Egress is behind you.
>
> But Bridgeward answers.
>
> The Tremorscope pulses once. The soot-black bead warms in your hand for a single breath.
>
> Then, from the lower passage ahead, stone answers stone.
>
> The Exodus Stair is behind you now.
>
> The bridge is next.

## Runtime Guardrails

Do not let this become a full hub exploration scene. Give the party:

1. One regroup check.
2. One route-reading check.
3. One side-route inspection if they ask.
4. One spirit echo.
5. The Soot-Black Prayer Bead.
6. Then transition toward Scene Packet 7.

Avoid adding combat here unless the party acts violently toward spirits or forcefully attacks sealed routes.

Do not resolve Lower Holds, Royal Descent, queen death, immortal king truth, or the final grief-source solution in this scene.

## Visual Direction for Map Reference

Use the canon Exodus Landing map as the visual anchor:

- Hand-drawn Roll20 tactical map style.
- Top-down, readable stone architecture.
- No baked grid.
- No labels.
- White or transparent-feeling background outside the map footprint.
- Rough dwarven stone walls with cracked tile floors.
- Central raised stone marker / route-control plinth.
- Four clear route exits.
- Dead lamp cups, lantern hooks, signal basins, and old sconce infrastructure.
- Broken rails and queue-control barriers.
- Scattered crates, collapsed benches, debris, and old route hardware.
- Subtle cursed crystal seams in blue, green-white, pale rose, and yellow tones.
- Restrained glow, not bright fantasy neon.
- Abandoned and functional, not treasure-filled or royal.
- Bridgeward should feel visually lower, darker, more formal, and more ominous than the other routes.

The strongest map-design rule: **this room is orientation, not exploration sprawl**. It gives the party a choice-shaped space, then tells them which route is alive with the story.
