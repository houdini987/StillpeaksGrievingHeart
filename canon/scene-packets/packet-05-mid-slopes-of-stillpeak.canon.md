# Scene Packet 5 - The Mid-Slopes of Stillpeak

## 1. At-a-Glance Summary

**Location:** 5 - The Mid-Slopes of Stillpeak

**Purpose:** Turn the ascent into a costly attrition threshold, prove Stillpeak is actively breaking minds and terrain, and point the party toward a hidden summit entrance leading down into the mountain.

**Expected Duration:** 45-75 minutes of play.

**Encounter Types:** Traversal under attack, technical climb, environmental pressure, hard threshold combat.

**Key Mechanics:** climbing strain, rope and anchor depletion, cold-impaired dexterity, altitude pressure, Tremorscope clue layering, unstable footing.

**Key Discoveries:** Stillpeak's vibrations are driving living creatures mad; prior climbers deliberately came this way; the Tremorscope indicates a hollow or focal void near the summit; a brief atmospheric reveal exposes a hidden seam at the summit that leads inward.

### Scene Purpose

Packet 5 is the mountain's attrition threshold.

By the time the party reaches these slopes, Stillpeak should stop feeling like a punishing route and start feeling like an active grinding force. Every segment should cost something: time, stability, blood, warmth, rope, pitons, certainty, or the confidence that open movement can still be treated casually.

This packet should:
- convert upward progress into expensive vertical gain
- prove the mountain's vibrations are destabilizing both minds and stone
- deplete climbing resources before the summit threshold fight
- reveal, with increasing clarity, that the summit conceals a deliberate entrance leading down into Stillpeak

### Design Intent

Maintain a coherent attrition arc rather than four isolated scenes.

Make each segment solve a different climbing problem with a distinct failure texture.

Let violence emerge from terrain and resonance pressure rather than feeling like unrelated wandering monsters.

End the packet with the party depleted, committed, and pointed directly at the summit breach.

### Segment I - Stonefold Ramps

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Stonefold Ramps reference art]

The first rise above the lower slopes looks survivable in the foolish way a bad idea can look survivable from a distance.

Then a slab the size of a wagon wheel shears past the party, smashes two shelves below, and vanishes into white.

The Stonefold Ramps are not a battlefield. They are a slanted stack of broken, ice-slick shelves that must be crossed while the mountain throws living ruin down from above.

Massive tilted plates of stone lean away from one another at wrong angles. Broken seams split each shelf into narrow traverses and abrupt drops. Snow has packed hard into the joints, then glazed over. Every step wants to slide sideways, and every pause invites another impact from higher up the slope.

#### Terrain & Atmosphere

- Massive stone shelves tilted just enough to ruin balance without looking unclimbable at a glance
- Broken seams and snow-packed joints that force awkward lateral traverses instead of clean upward movement
- Ice hidden beneath drifted powder, causing sudden slips, backward skids, and chain-fall risk
- Unstable higher shelves where impacts shake loose stone, old ice, and fresh debris onto the route below
- Exposure lanes with almost no natural cover, making stationary characters easier to target and easier to rattle

#### Mechanical Beats

- Treat this as a hybrid traversal-under-bombardment encounter, not a stand-up fight
- The real objective is movement: cross the ramps, secure the traverses, and keep the party from being knocked off line
- Advancing requires repeated Athletics or Acrobatics checks (**DC 13-15**) to cross slanted shelves, brace against impacts, arrest slides, or recover footing on iced stone
- Unsecured characters risk slips becoming chain-falls when one bad step or debris impact carries momentum across the shelf
- Rope, pitons, and anchor placements should matter immediately; parties that take time to secure lines reduce catastrophe risk but spend time under bombardment
- Characters who stop in the open to trade blows increase exposure and invite more falling stone, broken ice, and forced movement
- Valid solutions should remain flexible: secure and advance, suppress the giants long enough to advance, or split roles between defenders and line-setters
- Success should feel earned through coordination and gear use, not through simply killing everything on the map

#### Enemy Encounter - Vibration-Maddened Hill Giants

By default, 2 hill giants occupy unstable shelves above the route, though exact count can flex with party strength and map scale.

They are not guarding this place in any organized way. They are sleep-starved, disoriented, and visibly unraveling under Stillpeak's resonance. Their eyes track movement late, then too hard. They smash stone with their bare hands between attacks as if trying to break the pulses under their feet. At moments of stronger tremor, they react to the mountain itself more than to the party.

Run them as unstable bombardiers:
- they are dangerous because of size and instability, not discipline
- they hurl loose stone, shelf fragments, and shattered ice at exposed movement lanes rather than selecting targets cleanly
- they may waste actions battering the rock, roaring at unseen pulses, or overreacting to Tremorscope-like tremors
- they should make the slope more dangerous even when they are not landing direct hits
- suppressing them is useful, but fully defeating them is not the only path to success

#### Tremorscope

The Tremorscope does not merely jitter here. It bucks in sharp, overlapping surges whenever the giants scream, smash stone, or pause as if listening to something inside the slope.

Its readings make one ugly fact plain: the mountain's vibrations are not only destabilizing terrain. They are entering living minds, scrambling sleep, perception, and judgment until fear and pain become indistinguishable from command.

#### Narrative Signal

Stonefold Ramps is the party's commitment gate.

Stillpeak is now actively hostile. Stable footing is gone. Open movement is unsafe. Climbing gear is no longer optional expedition caution but the price of continuing at all.

### Segment II - The Wind-Carved Spine

The route narrows until it stops feeling like a slope at all.

The Wind-Carved Spine is a black-stone rib jutting out from the mountain like a blade only half-buried. Wind has stripped it clean of anything soft. No drifted snow. No broad shelves. No forgiving debris. Only dark stone, pale rime, and narrow holds that must be trusted one at a time.

This is the packet's revelation and commitment segment.

The climb turns the ascent from general survival into a precise objective. The party must divide roles, spend the most climbing support they have spent so far, and move in timed windows while the mountain slowly reveals that the summit is not merely the highest point. It is a sealed answer.

#### Environmental Hazards

- The ascent splits across narrow ribs, offset ledges, and short exposed traverses that prevent the party from climbing as one compact block
- Wind arrives in hard surges, creating brief movement windows followed by intervals where progress is reckless
- The stone is austere and wind-scoured; exposure is cleaner, emptier, and more precise than the chaos below
- Cold locks fingers and feet with painful numbness in all climbers except Erny, making fine motor work increasingly unreliable
- Anchor points degrade as the spine shifts under load, forcing re-placement, redundancy, and difficult decisions about where to spend support

#### Mechanical Beats

- Run this as a split-ascent technical climb under timed wind pressure, not as a combat encounter
- The party should naturally divide into roles such as lead climber or line-setter, anchor support, exposed climbers waiting for a movement window, and ranged overwatch or rescue positioning
- Athletics checks (**DC 15-17**) cover steep pulls, mantle points, controlled traverses, and holding body position when gusts hit early
- Survival or Perception checks (**DC 14-15**) read the wind's rhythm and identify the next safe movement interval
- Sleight-of-hand-adjacent climbing tasks, tool use, or equivalent checks can reflect numbed fingers when setting pitons, securing knots, or transferring weight to a fresh line; Erny is exempt from the worst cold-motor penalty
- Constitution saves (**DC 13-14**) reflect cold in hands and feet, breath loss, and exposure stress while waiting in bad positions for the next window
- This should be the largest gear sink in Packet 5: the most rope committed so far, the most piton and anchor placement so far, and the most time lost to setup, waiting, and re-securing compromised lines
- Failures should cost committed rope, spent anchors, forced re-rigging, dangerous pendulums, separated climbers, or rescued falls rather than simply dealing damage
- Confidence in fine motor movement should visibly erode over the segment; by the end, even simple securement should feel deliberate and expensive

#### Clue Layer A - Deliberate Prior Climbers

The first clue arrives in the stone itself.

As the party sets lines and searches for trustworthy holds, they begin finding signs that this exact approach was used before by climbers aiming for a very specific point high above:
- old piton scars hammered into black stone where no natural traveler would bother to place them
- weathered route traces that only make sense as a deliberate high-angle line
- a remnant of worked rope or fixed line frozen into a seam
- tool-cut notches or markers too intentional to be dismissed as coincidence

These traces should be discovered in motion, while choosing holds and placements, so the realization arrives as part of the climb: someone came here on purpose, and they were not merely trying to survive the mountain. They were trying to reach something.

#### Clue Layer B - Tremorscope Resonance Irregularity

Partway up the spine, the Tremorscope stops behaving like a simple uphill guide.

Its pulses begin to return strangely through the stone whenever the party stabilizes long enough to read it. The signal no longer feels like a single directional pull. It splits, delays, and answers itself from somewhere near the summit where solid rock should simply absorb it.

Arcana, Investigation, or Tremorscope interpretation checks (**DC 14-16**) should point toward the same conclusion:
- there is hidden internal structure near the summit rather than uninterrupted mass
- the resonance suggests a hollow, seam, or occluded cavity
- the summit is acting as a focus point, not merely the highest elevation
- what lies above is inside the mountain as much as on top of it

#### Clue Layer C - Telescopic Atmospheric Reveal

Only after the earlier clues are in place does the sky provide the payoff.

Crossing higher beyond the Karagvorn line, wind shear and resonance briefly align with the already-established atmospheric distortion. Blown ice and pale air bend into that impossible telescopic phenomenon for a few heartbeats.

In that moment, the summit face resolves with unnatural clarity.

Not for long. Just long enough.

A narrow seam appears in the crown of the peak: too clean, too recessed, too deliberate to be a natural break. It reads as a breach or concealed entrance before weather and distance swallow it again.

#### Narrative Signal

The party should leave the Wind-Carved Spine with three truths locked together:

Someone deliberately climbed toward the summit before them.

The Tremorscope can reveal hidden internal structure rather than merely pointing uphill.

The next objective is no longer abstract ascent. They must reach the summit, find the concealed seam, and enter the mountain through it.

### Segment III - The Shattered Moraine

Beyond the spine, the route broadens just enough to feel false.

The Shattered Moraine is a field of accumulated failure: shattered stone, ice-laced debris, buried collapse, and the half-kept remains of other attempts. Old anchors protrude from scree with nothing left attached. A snapped line emerges from frozen rubble and vanishes again. Here and there, bones or splintered gear show through the slope for a moment before the moraine shifts and half-buries them again.

Everything underfoot is moving, but never enough to trust where the motion will go next.

Altitude presses down like another loaded pack strapped over the shoulders. The air has thinned to the point where ordinary pace feels wasteful. Landmarks refuse to hold their shape correctly; what looks near may still take ten bitter minutes to reach, while a rise that seems minor steals breath like a wall.

This is the last grim barricade before the summit push, not a finale.

#### Terrain & Pressure

- Unstable scree and ice-laced debris move beneath the party, making even simple steps feel provisional
- The mountain's pressure manifests as added weight on bodies, packs, and weapon arms, as though grief itself has mass here
- Thin air imposes one level of low-oxygen strain; movement speed is reduced unless characters deliberately pace, rest, or spend resources to offset it
- Altitude and magical distortion make landmarks, distance, and safe lines harder to judge correctly
- Old collapses, exposed gear, and half-buried bones make the moraine feel less like wilderness and more like a place where prior attempts accumulated and stopped

#### Mechanical Beats

- Treat the full segment as a burdened final approach, not a simple crossing
- Movement occurs across unstable difficult terrain with settling pockets, rolling stone, and abrupt shifts in footing
- Group Survival or Athletics checks (**DC 14-15**) identify safer lines through the debris and determine who reaches key positions before the ground changes again
- The added sense of weight should matter without becoming bookkeeping: characters already burdened by Mountain's Toll, heavy gear, or prior attrition feel slower, more deliberate, and less able to recover from mistakes
- Thin air should have a visible tactical consequence by reducing movement speed and making repositioning, retreat, and rescue more expensive than normal
- Failures should cost pace, formation, or certainty: minor slides, bogged movement, separated allies, dropped footing, or bad starting positions when enemies surface
- The party should feel less capable than normal by the end of the segment, not because they are numerically shut down, but because the mountain has made every correction slower and every judgment less trustworthy

### Puzzle - The Silent Echo Cairn

**Placement:** Scene Packet 5 - Midway through Segment III (The Shattered Moraine), at a brief pocket of stillness in the moving field.

#### Read-Aloud Text

A ring of half-buried cairns interrupts the broken slope like something the mountain forgot to finish swallowing.

Five squat stacks of stone lean around a cracked central pillar. Snow clings in their seams. Old ice glazes their shaded sides. For the first time in what feels like hours, the ground inside the ring is almost still.

That stillness is not comforting.

It feels like held breath.

#### DM Notes

**Purpose:** A moment of interpretable silence inside the burden chamber, not a separate set piece competing with the segment's main function.

Use the cairn as a brief chance to read the mountain rather than outrun it. The party should feel that this pocket of stillness matters precisely because the rest of the moraine does not stay still.

#### Solution & Player Approaches

The Tremorscope reveals the cracked central pillar is resonating out of phase with the cairn ring.

The correct answer is still acceptance through fracture: topple the cracked pillar completely rather than trying to mend or stabilize it.

Repair attempts, excessive caution, or overhandling should cost time and reinforce the segment's burden.

#### Checks & Clues

- **Insight (DC 13):** The cracked pillar feels sorrowful and exhausted, not aggressive.
- **Nature (DC 14):** The ring once balanced forces around it; the broken center now drags everything slightly out of true.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** The Tremorscope syncs more cleanly when the central fracture is allowed to complete.
- **Athletics (DC 12):** The pillar can be toppled without collapsing the surrounding cairns.

#### Failure States

If the party spends too long trying to repair or decode the cairn, increase exposure time, worsen fatigue, or impose disadvantage on the next group movement check in the moraine.

If they destroy the whole ring carelessly, lose the reward and heighten the sense that the slope itself is listening.

#### Reward

**Dwarven Stone-Whistle (Harmonic Finder) (Uncommon; No attunement).**

This basalt whistle produces no audible note, only a vibration felt in the teeth. Once per long rest within the Karagvorn Line, you may blow it as an action. For 10 minutes, you have advantage on one check to interpret the Tremorscope or to navigate by terrain in poor visibility.

#### Enemy Encounter - Moraine-Bound Dead

Use summit-adjacent dead here: cairn-buried remnants, moraine-bound dead, or similar grave-cold climber remains that emerge from unstable scree and old collapse debris.

These should feel grim and transitional rather than climactic. They are the proof that many came this far and not all went farther.

Run them to punish slowed movement and uncertain footing:
- they surface from settling stone, exposed old gear fields, or bone-littered debris fans rather than charging from clear ground
- they close fastest on isolated or slowed characters who cannot easily reposition under low oxygen pressure
- they use the terrain to bog movement, force awkward retreats, and turn hesitation into contact
- they should feel like the mountain handing the party its own future in advance if they stop moving

#### Psychological Effects

- Landmarks seem to drift when not watched directly, making route judgment feel argumentative and fragile
- Voices carry badly; nearby words can sound far off while distant stone-settling sounds seem uncomfortably close
- Breath comes short enough to make simple planning clipped, irritable, and imprecise
- The mountain's weight and the altitude's distortion together make ordinary perception feel suspect in a way that is tiring, not spectacular

#### Camp Decision Point

This can still function as the last meaningful staging point before Packet 6, but it is a bad refuge rather than a good one. Rest here may steady the party for the summit push, yet it also means lingering under thin air, false distance, shifting debris, and the pressure that has already started settling into their bodies.

If the party camps here, emphasize that they are not recovering in safety. They are choosing to stop inside the compression chamber.

#### Narrative Signal

The Shattered Moraine should leave the party heavier, slower, and less certain than when they entered it.

The mountain is no longer only affecting terrain or distant minds. It is acting directly on the party through burden, thin air, and distortion.

This is the last grim barricade before the summit.

### Segment IV - The Ice-Locked Shelf

The final approach levels without becoming merciful.

At first glance, the Ice-Locked Shelf looks like relief: broad, open, almost orderly after the broken shelves, knife-edge climbs, and shifting moraine below. The surface is pale and clean. Frost has grown in geometric fans across black ice and buried stone. The summit wall rises ahead in hard planes and quiet angles, too deliberate to feel natural.

That relief is false.

The shelf is not here to drain the party of one more set of ropes and anchors. It is here to show them what it means to have arrived short on certainty, support, and room to recover.

#### Threshold Pressure

- The shelf presents only a few genuinely safe movement lanes once combat begins, and those lanes are narrow, slick, and easy to lose
- Repositioning is hazardous on the geometric ice; short movements can become slides, overcommits, or sudden separation
- Limited support resources from earlier segments should matter directly when considering spacing, rescue, retreat, and whether a bad displacement can be corrected
- Retreat options are poor and exposed, especially for parties that arrive with few anchors, little reserve rope, or reduced confidence in secure footing
- The environment should punish earlier depletion and low margin for error, not demand a fresh large-scale gear expenditure

### Puzzle - The Frozen Reflection Shelf

**Placement:** Scene Packet 5 - Immediately before or as the party first steps fully onto Segment IV (The Ice-Locked Shelf).

#### Read-Aloud Text

The ice ahead is smooth enough to reflect lanternlight, but the shapes inside it do not move with you at first.

For a breath, each reflection stands half a step behind its living body.

Older. More tired. Packs hanging lower. Shoulders bent as if the mountain has already finished with them.

Then the reflections catch up.

#### DM Notes

**Purpose:** Emotional and mechanical threshold that reinforces burden, not spectacle.

This moment should acknowledge what the climb has cost. It should soften the coming guardian encounter through positional or pressure relief, not by refunding the expedition scarcity the packet has been building.

#### Solution & Player Approaches

To open the safest path forward, a character either:
- sets down a meaningful burden respectfully, even for a moment, or
- speaks a brief sincere acknowledgment of loss, regret, fear, or obligation

The shelf answers admission, not domination.

#### Checks & Clues

- **Insight (DC 12):** The reflections emphasize burden and depletion, not deception.
- **Religion (DC 14):** The mountain's threshold behavior resembles an acknowledgment rite.
- **Arcana (DC 13):** This is resonance made visible through ice, not a normal illusion.

#### Failure States

If the party refuses the prompt and attempts to smash or bypass it, add exposure time, worsen their initial positioning, or deny them the mitigation effect against the guardian.

#### Reward

**Moment of Resonant Clarity.**

Instead of replenishing gear, the shelf grants a brief advantage of understanding. Once before the end of Segment IV, the party may invoke the shelf's answered resonance to do one of the following:
- reveal one safer movement lane across the ice for a short interval
- negate one forced slide, stumble, or separation effect caused by the shelf
- gain advantage on one check to hold footing, resist displacement, or reposition under pressure

This preserves the threshold's emotional payoff without undercutting Packet 5's attrition arc.

### Guardian Mitigation - Resonance of Acknowledgment

If the party engages the Frozen Reflection Shelf sincerely, apply one or more of the following mitigations to the guardian encounter:
- reduce the guardian's control over the shelf by slightly narrowing its area pressure
- create one safer lane of movement across the ice
- allow the Tremorscope to cancel or soften one speed-reducing, destabilizing, or separating effect during the fight

If the party rejects the threshold entirely, the guardian meets them at full strength.

### Combat Encounter - Grief-Bound Guardian

The Grief-Bound Guardian remains the packet's hard threshold encounter and the intentional manifestation of the mountain's grief.

This is not a flat arena fight. It is a scarcity-payoff encounter.

Recalibrate its movement and positioning pressure so earlier attrition matters immediately:
- scarce anchors and limited secure fallback options make edge control and recovery dangerous
- slower movement from prior fatigue, thin air, and moraine pressure turns repositioning into a real cost
- slippery geometry should make every shove, forced move, and lane denial feel worse for parties who arrived depleted
- any separation the guardian creates should feel threatening because the shelf offers little clutter to hide behind and few forgiving routes to reconnect
- the encounter should communicate that there is no comfortable staging ground left and no clean reset between surviving this moment and pushing into Packet 6

#### Intended Difficulty

- Hard encounter for a level 5 party of four, especially if earlier segments meaningfully taxed support and positioning tools
- Survivable, but tactically cruel to parties that arrive low on movement confidence, rescue options, or spacing discipline
- The difficulty should come from scarcity, displacement, and lane pressure as much as from raw damage output

#### Tremorscope

The Tremorscope steadies into a hard doubled pulse on the shelf: the mountain's body below, the hidden hollow above, and the guardian standing between them like a locked hinge.

During the fight, it should feel less like a warning instrument and more like a witness to the mountain's attention.

If the guardian falls, the device clears just long enough to indicate direction rather than danger.

Upward. Toward the summit seam.

#### Narrative Signal

The Ice-Locked Shelf should feel like false relief becoming judgment.

The mountain has fully noticed the party. Earlier attrition now has immediate tactical consequences. There is no comfortable place left to regroup before Packet 6.

The packet ends with the party diminished, the summit entrance fixed as the only meaningful objective, and the sense that Stillpeak has stopped testing whether they will continue and started testing what they have left to spend.

## 3. Read-Aloud Scene Introduction

Stillpeak steepens without warning.

Not because the slope suddenly becomes impossible, but because every upward yard starts costing more than it should.

The shelves lean wrong. Ice hides where stone should hold. Wind worries at every loose strap and exposed hand. Even before the first impact, the mountain feels less like a place to cross and more like a weight being applied.

The Tremorscope does not calm here.

It shivers, catches, answers itself.

As though the mountain is no longer only beneath your feet.

As though something inside it has started listening back.

## 4. Environment Overview

- **Terrain:** slanted stone shelves, exposed ridgeline, unstable moraine, frozen summit threshold.
- **Atmosphere:** pressured silence, costly verticality, cold that numbs technique before it kills outright.
- **Visibility:** intermittent and deceptive; brief moments of impossible clarity matter more than steady sightlines.

## 5. Running the Location

Run Packet 5 as a linked attrition climb.

Keep pressure cumulative. When the party spends rope, loses footing, drops pace, or arrives colder and less certain than before, carry that forward. The packet works best if each segment narrows the margin for error in the next one.

Do not let combat fully halt the climb. Even in hostile encounters, the mountain remains the primary problem.

## 6. Encounter Modules

- **Stonefold Ramps:** traversal under bombardment by vibration-maddened hill giants.
- **Wind-Carved Spine:** technical split climb with wind timing, cold impairment, and summit-breach clue layering.
- **Shattered Moraine:** unstable approach pressure with a harassment encounter that exploits slowed movement.
- **Ice-Locked Shelf:** hard threshold fight where earlier depletion makes lanes, repositioning, and recovery immediately dangerous.

## 7. Player Curiosity Hooks

- Old tool marks and failed climbing traces suggest someone deliberately sought the summit.
- Tremorscope readings imply a hidden interior void rather than a merely dangerous peak.
- A brief atmospheric reveal exposes a seam or door-like break high near the summit crown.
- Living creatures encountered here show that Stillpeak's resonance is actively destroying sanity.

## 8. Escalation Signal

End the packet with a strong directional confirmation: the Tremorscope stabilizes on the summit breach, the weather parts just enough to reassert the seam, or the party finds one final deliberate climbing trace aimed straight at the hidden entrance.

## 9. Rewards / Discoveries

- Hard confirmation that the summit contains a concealed entrance leading down into Stillpeak.
- Practical lessons about conserving and spending climbing resources under pressure.
- Optional threshold mitigation and momentary clarity that matter because the party is already running low.

## 10. DM Reference Sidebar

Stillpeak expresses grief through pressure, silence, distortion, and the conversion of effort into cost.

Packet 5 should feel like the mountain discovering how to spend the party's strength faster than they can replace it.
