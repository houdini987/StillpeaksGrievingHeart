# Scene Packet 5 — The Mid-Slopes of Stillpeak

## 1. At-a-Glance Summary

**Location:** 5 — The Mid-Slopes of Stillpeak

**Purpose:** Advance the Stillpeak narrative and introduce key environmental or investigative elements.

**Expected Duration:** 30–60 minutes of play.

**Encounter Types:** Exploration, roleplay, environmental tension.

**Key Mechanics:** Cold, navigation uncertainty, tremor signals.

**Key Discoveries:** Clues about the mountain’s sorrow and spreading influence.

---

## 2. Narrative Setup (Canonical Text)

This scene packet advances the party’s ascent using encounter-anchored terrain segments. Movement is slow, visibility is limited, and the mountain’s grief becomes increasingly oppressive. Combat appears deliberately, not constantly, reinforcing that the environment itself is the primary antagonist.

### Design Intent

Cluster encounters around distinct geological features rather than abstract distance.

Escalate pressure through cold, encumbrance, and altitude without overwhelming the party.

Introduce combat as punctuation, not routine.

Reinforce the Tremorscope as the only consistently reliable guide.

### Segment I — The Stonefold Ramps (No Combat)

[IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Stonefold Ramps reference art]

The ascent begins across massive, tilted stone shelves pushed upward like broken plates. Snow fills the seams between slabs, and wind funnels unpredictably through narrow gaps. The climb is not technically difficult, but it is relentless.

#### Terrain & Atmosphere

- Uneven, slanted stone surfaces coated in wind-packed snow
- Sudden gusts that tug at packs and cloaks
- Long sightlines downward, limited sight upward

#### Mechanical Beats

- Difficult terrain for characters carrying at or above normal encumbrance
- Athletics or Acrobatics checks (**DC 12–14**) to maintain footing
- Failure causes delay and increased exposure time, not damage
- Cold exposure is narrative only at this stage

#### Tremorscope

The Tremorscope displays thin, steady lines with occasional gentle vibrations, like a distant heartbeat. It subtly leans uphill, though the path is not yet obvious.

#### Narrative Signal

The mountain resists passage through weight and friction, not violence. It feels aware but uninterested—an obstacle rather than an adversary.

### Segment II — The Wind-Carved Spine (Combat: Easy)

The path narrows into an exposed ridgeline where constant wind has scoured ice from black stone ribs. Gusts scream through the gaps like breath forced through broken teeth.

#### Environmental Hazards

- High winds impose disadvantage on ranged attacks beyond normal range
- Perception checks relying on sound are made at disadvantage
- Forced movement risks sliding 10–20 feet toward ledges

#### Combat Encounter — Stone-Bound Wights

2–4 Stone-Bound Wights animate as the party traverses the exposed stone. These undead are slow, brittle, and symbolic—warnings rather than hunters.

#### Intended Difficulty

- Low hit points and predictable attacks
- Primary danger comes from positioning and wind, not raw damage
- Designed to be won cleanly and quickly

#### Tremorscope

During combat, the Tremorscope pulses sharply. Each wight’s destruction causes a brief spike, followed by unsettling calm.

#### Narrative Signal

The mountain has responded for the first time. The violence feels reflexive, not malicious.

### Segment III — The Shattered Moraine (No Combat)

Beyond the ridge lies a wide field of broken stone and ice. Moraine debris shifts underfoot, and every step sinks, slides, or grinds. Sound is swallowed completely.

#### Terrain & Pressure

- Difficult terrain for all creatures
- Visibility reduced by drifting ice crystals
- Navigation landmarks indistinct and unreliable

### Puzzle — The Silent Echo Cairn

**Placement:** Scene Packet 5 — Segment III (The Shattered Moraine), mid-way through the moraine field.

#### Read-Aloud Text

A ring of half-buried cairns breaks the monotony of shattered stone—five squat stacks surrounding a cracked pillar at the center. Each cairn bears a simple symbol: stone, ice, fire, root, and heart. The air is so still your own breathing feels muffled.

#### DM Notes

**Purpose:** Stillpeak ‘speaks’ through silence; grief resolves through acknowledgment, not repair.

Place the Tremorscope in the center to ‘conduct’ faint vibrations through the cairns.

#### Solution & Player Approaches

The Tremorscope’s pulse reveals the cracked central pillar resonates out of phase.

The correct solution is to topple the cracked pillar completely (accept fracture rather than deny it).

Repair attempts deepen dissonance and waste time.

#### Checks & Clues

- **Insight (DC 13):** the ‘wrong’ pillar feels sorrowful, not dangerous; it wants release.
- **Nature (DC 14):** the symbols represent balance; the cracked pillar interrupts the cycle.
- **Arcana (DC 15):** the Tremorscope syncs to the ring; the cracked pillar produces dissonance.
- **Athletics (DC 12):** topple the pillar safely without destroying the ring.

#### Failure States

If the party spends 10+ minutes trying to fix it, increase travel time by 1 hour and impose disadvantage on the next navigation check.

If the ring is destroyed, the reward is lost and the ‘being watched’ sensation intensifies through the next segment.

#### Reward

**Dwarven Stone-Whistle (Harmonic Finder) (Uncommon; No attunement).**

This basalt whistle produces no audible note, only a vibration felt in the teeth. Once per long rest within the Karagvorn Line, you may blow it as an action. For 10 minutes, you have advantage on one check to interpret the Tremorscope or to navigate by terrain in poor visibility.

#### Mechanical Beats

- Group Survival check (**DC 13**) to find the least punishing route
- Failure increases travel time and exposure, not damage
- Encumbrance penalties begin affecting even lightly loaded characters

#### Psychological Effects

- Voices feel muted, even at close range
- Compasses drift; simple guidance magic gives technically correct but misleading routes
- The Tremorscope becomes the only trusted reference point

#### Camp Decision Point

This is the most defensible location to establish a camp before the final push. Resting here reduces later risk but increases total time spent exposed to the elements.

#### Narrative Signal

The mountain is no longer resisting—it is wearing the party down through silence and uncertainty.

### Puzzle — The Frozen Reflection Shelf

**Placement:** Scene Packet 5 — Immediately before Segment IV (The Ice-Locked Shelf).

#### Read-Aloud Text

A smooth wall of ice rises ahead like a sheet of glass. It reflects your lanternlight—but not your faces. In the ice, each of you looks older, more tired, your packs heavier than they should be. For a heartbeat, the reflection seems to breathe back.

#### DM Notes

**Purpose:** emotional gate that can mechanically soften the Grief-Bound Guardian fight without forcing melodrama.

Attacking/melting is ineffective; the mountain responds to voluntary burden and acknowledgment.

#### Solution & Player Approaches

To open the passage, a character either sets down a meaningful item respectfully or speaks a sincere acknowledgment of regret/loss/burden (brief is enough).

If the party engages sincerely, the ice fractures into a safe passage and the guardian is mitigated.

#### Checks & Clues

- **Insight (DC 12):** reflections emphasize burden, not threat.
- **Religion (DC 14):** dwarven myth—mountain bones demand acknowledgment before passage.
- **Arcana (DC 13):** resonance, not conventional illusion; the ice is a mirror for grief.

#### Failure States

If the party refuses and brute-forces alternatives, add 1–2 hours of exposure and impose disadvantage on the first initiative roll in Segment IV.

#### Reward

**Frost-Tempered Piton Set (Uncommon; No attunement).**

Six dull-silver pitons and braided cord resistant to brittleness. As an action, you may anchor the pitons to create a secure climbing line. The next creature to climb using the line has advantage on one Athletics check to climb, resist slipping, or arrest a fall. The pitons remain until removed.

### Guardian Mitigation — Resonance of Acknowledgment

The Grief-Bound Guardian is bound by resonance, not hatred. If the party resolves the Frozen Reflection Shelf sincerely (by setting down a meaningful burden or speaking an acknowledgment), apply the mitigation effects below when the guardian manifests.

- Reduce the guardian’s Aura of Oppressive Stillness radius by 5 feet.
- Reduce the guardian’s AC by 1 (cracks form in the ice-plated stone).
- Once during the fight, when the guardian would impose a speed-reducing effect, the Tremorscope emits a stabilizing pulse: one creature of the party’s choice ignores that single speed reduction.

If the party bypasses or refuses the puzzle, the guardian manifests at full strength.

### Segment IV — The Ice-Locked Shelf (Combat: Hard)

A broad, frozen shelf spreads beneath a towering ice wall. Frost forms unnatural geometric patterns, and the air feels heavy, almost thick. This place feels intentional.

#### Environmental Pressure

- Cold becomes mechanically relevant
- Slips, falling ice, and collapsing stone possible
- Exhaustion checks may occur after combat, not during

#### Combat Encounter — Grief-Bound Guardian

A single Grief-Bound Guardian rises from the shelf itself—stone and ice drawn together by sorrow. This is not a random defender, but a manifestation of the mountain’s grief.

#### Intended Difficulty

- A difficult fight for a level 5 party of four
- Survivable, but resource-draining
- Signals clearly that greater danger lies ahead

#### Tremorscope

The Tremorscope vibrates constantly throughout the encounter. Upon the guardian’s defeat, it emits one long, steady pulse, then falls silent for a moment.

#### Narrative Signal

The mountain has fully noticed the party. Survival here does not bring relief—only grim understanding.

- Question locals or examine written records.
- Study environmental disturbances or wildlife absence.
