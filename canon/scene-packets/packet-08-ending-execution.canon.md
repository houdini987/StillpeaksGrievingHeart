## Locked Ending Decisions

### Ending Q1 — The Mercy Plea

The King's mercy plea should be **poetic but clear**.

He should not bluntly say "kill me" or "end it," or turn the ending into a mechanical instruction. The meaning should still be unmistakable: he understands that continued preservation is the catastrophe, and he is asking for release.

### Ending Q2 — The Release

The release after mercy death or successful relic release is **layered, restrained, and split between the characters**:

1. A sad silence falls over the chamber.
2. The green light drains out of the crystal, from the throne outward.
3. **Erny and Maelreth** feel the mountain let go. The hum they have carried stops. The green afterglow still swims in their eyes, so they do not see clearly what happens next.
4. **Alina, Sorin, and Bilbo**, who were never attuned, see it plainly: the queen's procession from the Queen's Bridge appears one last time, and this time it finishes its crossing. The queen, in pale ceremonial cloth, reaches the far side and walks on with her people.
5. The spirits across the chamber lower their heads, and the old repeating loops stop.
6. The King ends as a mortal man. Stillpeak goes silent.

This is the completion of an interrupted rite, not a reunion. The queen does not speak, does not turn to the King, and does not come back. The King does not reach for her or ask her to stay. No one in the room should mistake this for undoing what happened.

**Why it is split:** the unattuned characters own the campaign's last image. The attuned pair get the physical end of the burden they have carried. Every character at the table gets a line addressed to them.

**Fallbacks:**

- If the Queen's Bridge procession memory did not land at the table, use the queen alone: a pale-clothed dwarven woman seen for one breath behind the broken throne, walking away, then gone.
- If Erny or Maelreth is down or absent, give the remaining attuned character the full line. If both are down, skip that paragraph.
- If none of Alina, Sorin, or Bilbo is conscious, the attuned characters see the procession instead as the green finally clears from their eyes.
- Bilbo is the DM's character. Address him in the line, but let Alina and Sorin carry any player reaction.

## Spoken-Word Style

Ending read-aloud should sound like a DM speaking to people at the table, not like formal fantasy verse.

Prioritize:

- plain emotional language
- short but natural sentences
- clear cause and effect
- grief without melodrama
- satisfaction without victory-cheering
- closure for the King and the Queen without reunion

Avoid:

- stacked poetic fragments
- abstract declarations
- repeated "not as / not as" constructions
- language that makes the ending feel generated or over-written
- queen dialogue, reunion speeches, or resurrection framing

The ending may be mythic in consequence, but the words at the table should be human.

## Clarity Window Purpose

The clarity window begins when both green beams break and Dhurak and Veyra can no longer hold the King as the vessel of their preservation engine.

At that moment, mechanically:

- Druid interception ends.
- Attuned Offense Backlash ends.
- The recurring Final Attunement Crisis ends.
- Beam damage absorption on the King ends.

The scene should do four things quickly:

1. Show the King is mortal and no longer the mountain's voice.
2. Confirm he understands enough to ask for release.
3. Give the party room for mercy death or the narrow relic-salvation attempt.
4. Prevent the table from drifting into a long negotiation loop.

Do not over-explain the lore here. The players should already understand the tragedy before this moment.

---

## Clarity Window Read-Aloud

Use when the second beam breaks.

> The second beam snaps.
>
> For the first time since you entered, the geode chamber is not humming.
>
> The King gasps. Not like a monster. Like an old man waking up in a room he does not recognize.
>
> His eyes move across the broken crystal, the shattered Druids, the throne that has held him, and the blast-scarred chamber around him.
>
> When he speaks, it is just his voice. The mountain is not speaking through him anymore.
>
> "What did I do?"

## King's Mercy Plea — Poetic but Clear

Use two to four of these lines. Do not monologue.

> "Where is she? No. No, I know. I knew. I would not know."

> "Do not let me become the mountain again."

> "If there is mercy left in this place, let it be swift."

> "I tried to keep death from taking her. Instead I taught it to wait for everyone else."

> "I would have called it love, once. It was not."

> "Let the mountain release what I would not."

> "Before the grief remembers how to wear my voice — finish what the beams began to break."

These lines should make the request obvious without the King saying "kill me." Then ask the table what they do.

## If the Party Hesitates

If the party is emotionally processing, give them room.

If hesitation becomes a table stall, use one pressure cue:

> A thin green pulse crawls through the cracked throne.
>
> The King feels it before anyone else does. His eyes widen.
>
> "No. Not again. Please — not again."

Then prompt for a decision.

Do not punish one short conversation. Do not allow indefinite debate.

---

## Refusal / Delay

If the party refuses to act, tries to preserve the King indefinitely, or misunderstands mercy as a failure state:

- crystal remnants begin to pulse again
- the King panics
- the grief-engine attempts to reassert itself
- the DM may force a final decision or restart a reduced final pressure scene

Suggested cue:

> The cracked throne gives one low pulse.
>
> Then another.
>
> The King grips the broken crystal with bloodied hands.
>
> "It is remembering me."
>
> His voice breaks.
>
> "Do not let it finish."

## Mercy Death

Mercy death is the cleanest and most supported ending.

If the party grants mercy during the clarity window, the King dies as a mortal man, not as a monster. Then run the release below.

### Mercy Death and Release Read-Aloud

Read the bracketed names aloud as written. Look at each player as you address their character.

> The King goes still.
>
> For a moment, nothing else happens.
>
> Then the room gets quiet in a way it has not been quiet before.
>
> The throne cracks under him. The green light drains out of the crystal veins, first around the throne, then across the floor, then up the walls.
>
> The pressure in your skulls eases. The air feels heavy, but it is only air now.

**To Erny and Maelreth:**

> Erny. Maelreth. The hum in your teeth stops.
>
> You have carried it so long you stopped noticing it. Now it is gone, and the quiet where it used to be is almost too loud.
>
> The mountain is not pulling at you anymore. It let go.
>
> Green afterglow still swims across your eyes. You can tell something is moving behind the throne, but you cannot make it out.

**To Alina, Sorin, and Bilbo:**

> Alina. Sorin. Bilbo. Your eyes are clear, and you see it.
>
> Behind the broken throne, the procession from the bridge is walking again. Lanterns. Banners. Bowed heads. The same people you watched cross the span.
>
> At the center walks the queen, in her pale ceremonial cloth.
>
> This time nobody stumbles. Nothing shakes.
>
> The procession reaches the far side and keeps going, through the crystal wall as if it were an open door.
>
> The queen does not look back.

**To everyone:**

> Around the chamber, the spirits lower their heads. No one cheers. No one speaks.
>
> One by one, the old sounds stop: the running footsteps, the counting, the prayers, the same last moments repeating over and over.
>
> The King looks peaceful now. Not happy. Not forgiven for everything. But finished.
>
> The last green light goes out, and Stillpeak is silent.

## Slim Relic Salvation

Salvation is possible but narrow. It is not the expected ending and should not be signposted.

Only these queen-linked mourning/restraint relics count:

1. **Soot-Black Prayer Bead**
2. **Torn White Procession Cloth**

The Queen's Lantern Charm does not count for salvation, though it helps with attunement/fear pressure (see `packet-08-attunement-affliction.canon.md`).

Check the session log before assuming the party holds either relic (LL-004).

The relic must be used during the clarity window.

Suggested check:

- One character presents or invokes the relic with **Religion, Persuasion, or Arcana DC 18**.
- Advantage if the party frames the act as release, not restoration.
- Advantage if Erny or Maelreth helps despite over-attunement echoes.
- Failure collapses back toward mercy death; it never creates a campaign dead end.

Possible success result:

- the King is separated from the mountain without the party killing him
- he becomes mortal and dies naturally in that moment or a few breaths later, as himself
- Stillpeak begins to return to neutrality
- the queen is not resurrected
- the King does not receive a happy ending; he receives release from being a catastrophe

### Relic Presented

> The relic does not blaze.
>
> It just holds steady in the green light, which in this room feels almost impossible.

### Salvation Failure, Mercy Still Open

> The relic trembles in your hand.
>
> It is not enough to save him.
>
> But it is enough for him to understand why he has to end.

Then return to the mercy plea and the Mercy Death read-aloud.

### Successful Relic Release Read-Aloud

> The relic steadies in the green light.
>
> The throne pulls against it for one hard breath.
>
> Then something gives.
>
> The crystal fused through the King's crown and hands dulls from green to gray. He slumps forward, breathing like a man who has just come up from deep water.
>
> The room gets quiet in a way it has not been quiet before.

**To Erny and Maelreth:** use the same lines as in the Mercy Death read-aloud.

**To Alina, Sorin, and Bilbo:**

> Alina. Sorin. Bilbo. Your eyes are clear, and you see it.
>
> Behind the broken throne, the procession from the bridge is walking again. Lanterns. Banners. Bowed heads.
>
> At the center walks the queen, in her pale ceremonial cloth.
>
> This time nobody stumbles. The procession reaches the far side and keeps going, through the crystal wall as if it were an open door.

**To everyone:**

> The King sees it too.
>
> He does not reach for her. He does not ask her to stay.
>
> He only closes his eyes.
>
> Around the chamber, the spirits lower their heads. No one cheers. No one speaks. The old sounds stop, one by one.
>
> Whether he dies in that moment or a few breaths later, he dies as himself.
>
> When the last lantern passes through the wall, the mountain lets go.

## After the Release — The King's Pendant

After the King dies or is fully released, the party may inspect his body and the throne.

> The crown and throne are ruined, but the pendant around his neck is still intact.
>
> It is a small green crystal on a blackened chain.
>
> After everything you have seen in this mountain, you know right away that it is not just jewelry.

This is **the First Green Heart**. For identification, attunement, properties, and epilogue handling, use `packet-08-first-green-heart-artifact.canon.md`.

## Emotional Target

The final feeling should be sad satisfaction.

The party should feel that:

- the King made terrible choices
- the Druids' corruption weaponized his grief
- the Queen was never truly recoverable
- mercy was the right answer
- Stillpeak is still ruined, but no longer trapped
- the Queen's interrupted crossing is finally complete, and the King's grief is finally allowed to end
