# Scene Packet 8 Supplement — Ending Execution

## Canon Status

This supplement is canon for Scene Packet 8 / Chapter 8 and governs the King's clarity window, mercy-death plea, slim relic salvation handling, refusal/delay pressure, and final release narration.

## Ending Q1 Locked Decision

The King's mercy plea should be **poetic but clear**.

He should not bluntly say "kill me" or turn the ending into a mechanical instruction. The meaning should still be unmistakable: he understands that continued preservation is the catastrophe, and he is asking for release.

---

## Clarity Window Purpose

The clarity window begins when both green beams break and Dhurak/Veyra can no longer hold the King as the vessel of their preservation engine.

The scene should do four things quickly:

1. Show the King is mortal and no longer the mountain's voice.
2. Confirm he understands enough to ask for release.
3. Give the party room for mercy death or the narrow relic-salvation attempt.
4. Prevent the table from drifting into a long negotiation loop.

Do not over-explain the lore here. The players should already understand the tragedy before this moment.

---

## Clarity Window Read-Aloud

Use when the second beam breaks.

> The second beam snaps.
>
> For the first time since you entered, the geode chamber is not humming.
>
> The king gasps.
>
> Not like a monster.
>
> Like an old man waking in a room he does not recognize.
>
> His eyes move across the broken crystal, the shattered Druids, the throne that has held him, and the round blast-scar of the chamber around him.
>
> When he speaks, the mountain is no longer speaking for him.
>
> "What did I do?"

---

## King's Mercy Plea — Poetic but Clear

Use two to four of these lines. Do not monologue.

> "Do not let me become the mountain again."

> "If there is mercy left in this place, let it be swift."

> "I tried to keep death from taking her. Instead I taught it to wait for everyone else."

> "I would have called it love, once. It was not."

> "Let the mountain release what I would not."

> "Before the grief remembers how to wear my voice — finish what the beams began to break."

These lines should make the request obvious without the King saying "kill me."

---

## If the Party Hesitates

If the party is emotionally processing, give them room.

If hesitation becomes table-stall, use one pressure cue:

> A thin green pulse crawls through the cracked throne.
>
> The king feels it before anyone else does.
>
> His eyes widen.
>
> "No. Not again. Please — not again."

Then prompt for a decision.

Do not punish one short conversation. Do not allow indefinite debate.

---

## Mercy Death Execution

Mercy death is the cleanest and most supported ending.

If the party grants mercy during the clarity window:

- the King dies as a mortal man, not as a monster
- the central throne cracks
- green light drains from the radial crystal veins
- the mountain's grief-pressure begins to release
- trapped spirits may exhale, bow, or fade
- the queen's memory may appear briefly as release, not reunion

Suggested cue:

> The king dies like a mortal.
>
> Not as stone.
> Not as thunder.
> Not as a law the mountain must obey.
>
> Just a grieving man, finally allowed to end.

---

## Slim Relic Salvation Execution

Salvation is possible but narrow.

Only these queen-linked mourning/restraint relics count:

1. **Soot-Black Prayer Bead**
2. **Torn White Procession Cloth**

The Queen's Lantern Charm does not count for salvation, though it may help with attunement/fear pressure if the DM wants.

The relic must be used during the clarity window.

Suggested check:

- One character presents or invokes the relic with **Religion, Persuasion, or Arcana DC 18**.
- Advantage if the party frames the act as release, not restoration.
- Advantage if Erny or Maelreth helps despite over-attunement echoes.
- Failure collapses back toward mercy death; it should not create a total campaign dead end.

Possible success result:

- the King is separated from the mountain without immediate killing
- he becomes mortal, dying naturally shortly afterward or surviving only long enough to bless release
- Stillpeak begins to return to neutrality
- the queen is not resurrected
- the King does not receive a happy ending; he receives release from being a catastrophe

---

## Refusal / Delay

If the party refuses to act, tries to preserve the King indefinitely, or misunderstands mercy as a failure state:

- crystal remnants begin to pulse again
- the King panics
- the grief-engine attempts to reassert itself
- the DM may force a final decision or restart a reduced final pressure scene

Suggested cue:

> The cracked throne gives one low pulse.
>
> Then another.
>
> The king grips the broken crystal with bloodied hands.
>
> "It is remembering me."
>
> His voice breaks.
>
> "Do not let it finish."

---

## Final Release Cue

Use after mercy death or successful relic release.

> The crystals do not shatter all at once.
>
> They dim like embers denied air.
>
> Far away, somewhere above and below you at the same time, the mountain exhales.
>
> The spirits do not cheer.
>
> They lower their heads.
>
> Then, one by one, they stop repeating.
