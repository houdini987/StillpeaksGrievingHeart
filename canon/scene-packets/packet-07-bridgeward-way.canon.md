## 2. Narrative Setup

The party leaves the Exodus Stair and follows the Bridgeward route into the deeper heart of Stillpeak. The chapter should feel like the mountain widening from survival infrastructure into a dead civilization.

The city is mostly seen, not crawled. The point is scale: Stillpeak was not a dungeon. It was a kingdom. Its roads, balconies, bridges, halls, shrines, and civic stonework remain, but ordinary life is gone.

The cursed crystal seams are no longer background light. They pulse with something closer to attention. The closer the party gets to the Queen's Bridge, the more spirits appear. These spirits are not hostile by default. They are memory-bound witnesses and victims, reliving sacred procession, sudden tremor, failed rescue, and immediate aftermath.

Chapter 7's emotional thesis:

**The party crosses the place where the mountain learned to grieve.**

## 4. Environment Overview

### Spatial Model

Packet 7 should use a controlled map footprint:

1. **Bridgeward Threshold / Empty City Verge** — short route and scenic overlook.
2. **Witness Halls** — compact 4–5 area playable dungeon.
3. **Queen's Bridge** — major set-piece crossing and combat map.
4. **Far-Side Mourning Threshold** — short closing scene beyond the bridge.

Do not build a sprawling city crawl. The city should feel immense and inaccessible, not fully explorable.

### Light and Crystal Attention

Continue Packet 6's light logic: Stillpeak was built for ordinary dwarven light, but the cursed seam-glow now illuminates it. In Packet 7, the crystals become more responsive.

Use escalating descriptions:

- Early Packet 7: crystals pulse faintly when the Tremorscope beats.
- Witness Halls: crystals brighten near memory and grief-related scenes.
- Animus encounter: crystals become body and motion.
- Queen's Bridge: crystals flare like opened eyes when the spirits notice the party.
- Far-Side Threshold: crystals pulse along the throneward route as invitation or warning.

### Spirit Density

Spirits become more numerous closer to the Queen's Bridge. They should be:

- memory-bound, not socially available by default
- tragic, not combat targets
- useful as directional and emotional clues
- strongest near the bridge and far-side aftermath

### Mountain Attunement

Erny and Maelreth's attunement becomes more costly in Chapter 7.

In upper Packet 6, attunement was mostly useful. In Packet 7, it is intrusive:

- pressure behind the eyes
- hum in the teeth
- sympathy toward grief-crystal creatures
- false certainty
- brief emotional blending with memory-echoes
- disadvantage against grief-crystal sympathy mechanics where specified

Do not use this to remove agency. Use it to create meaningful pressure and spotlight.

## 5. Running the Chapter

### Pacing Rule

Chapter 7 should move through escalating certainty:

1. The party knows Bridgeward is important.
2. The city shows this was a kingdom.
3. Witness Halls show the bridge was sacred/civic and spiritually dangerous.
4. The bridge reveals the queen's fall clearly.
5. The far side points toward the king and throneward descent.

### Reveal Boundary

The queen's fall should become clear in Chapter 7. The final grief solution should not.

State clearly through the bridge memory:

- The queen fell because of a tremor.
- The fall happened during a sacred/civic procession.
- The fall was witnessed.
- The immediate aftermath involved chaos, failed rescue attempts, and efforts to warn the king.

Do not state:

- the king is immortal
- the mountain-heart is the anchor
- the method for ending the curse
- the whole causal chain between grief and mountain corruption

### Geomancer Boundary

Do not frame Chapter 7 as a geomancer failure. Geomancers are a separate order and are not the dwarven religious/civic authority behind this bridge tragedy.

## 6. Encounter Modules

### BEAT 1 — Bridgeward Threshold

**Purpose:** Transition from Exodus Landing into the deeper city route.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Following Bridgeward signs and feeling the route change from evacuation infrastructure to formal civic/ceremonial approach.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Bridgeward markers are official route signs, not warning graffiti.
- **History DC 14:** The route likely led to a ceremonial or civic crossing.
- **Arcana or Religion DC 15:** The resonance is stronger because the route approaches a major grief-memory site.
- **Survival DC 13:** The route is unstable but passable.

**Success:** The party understands they are entering a formal interior route, not another emergency stair.

**Failure:** They still proceed, but the first glimpse of the city must do more orientation work.

**Spoken Cue:**

> Bridgeward Way does not feel abandoned in the way the Exodus Stair did.
>
> The stair was a wound people climbed out of.
>
> This passage feels like a vein leading inward.

---

### BEAT 2 — Empty City Verge

**Purpose:** Reveal the dead dwarven city without creating a city crawl.

**What the Party Is Doing:** Reaching an overlook, broken balcony, or transit platform above the lifeless interior city.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Perception DC 13:** Notice faint spirit-shapes moving in loops far below.
- **Investigation DC 14:** The district was evacuated or sealed, not destroyed by invasion.
- **History DC 15:** The route geometry suggests the bridge was a major civic/ceremonial crossing.
- **Arcana DC 15:** The crystals pulse more strongly when watched too long.
- **Insight DC 13:** The emptiness feels preserved, not merely abandoned.

**Success:** The party understands Stillpeak was a real city/kingdom and that Bridgeward leads toward a central meaningful span.

**Failure:** The party still sees the scale, but may read the city as ordinary ruin until Witness Halls clarify the ceremonial route.

**Read-Aloud:**

> The passage opens onto height.
>
> Not outside. Not sky.
>
> A hollow vastness inside the mountain.
>
> Below you, the dwarven city descends in terraces: silent roads, dark rooftops, sealed halls, market squares with no merchants, bridges connecting districts no living foot has crossed in ages.
>
> It is not ruined in the ordinary way.
>
> It is too intact for that.
>
> Nothing moves in the streets except pale spirit-shapes repeating paths they no longer understand.
>
> Nothing breathes.
>
> Only the crystals move.

**Forward Motion Cue:**

> The Tremorscope beats again.
>
> Across the dead city, a line of crystal seams brightens one after another, marking a route toward a darker ceremonial arch.
>
> Bridgeward.

### BEAT 5 — Far-Side Mourning Threshold

**Purpose:** Short post-bridge scene and chapter endpoint.

The far side should not become a full dungeon. It should point toward Chapter 8.

**Suggested Checks:**

- **Dwarvish DC 12:** Read a marker indicating a lower royal/throneward route.
- **History DC 14:** Understand the passage is royal/civic, not residential.
- **Religion DC 14:** Recognize dropped objects as sacred/processional remnants.
- **Insight DC 13:** Spirits are reliving immediate aftermath, not guarding the passage.
- **Perception DC 13:** Notice spirits rushing downward with repeated phrases about warning the king or reaching the cavern floor.

**Spirit Fragments:**

> "Tell the king—no, not like that—do not let him hear it from the bridge."

> "Ropes. Lower ropes. She fell below the lantern reach."

> "No one goes alone. The cavern is too deep."

> "Throneward. Someone must tell him. Someone must survive telling him."

> "Do not say her name while running. Do not carry grief before the throne."

**Chapter Endpoint Read-Aloud:**

> The last spirit does not flee toward the bridge.
>
> He runs deeper.
>
> Down a stair marked by a cracked royal sigil, clutching a torn strip of white procession cloth in both hands.
>
> "Throneward," he whispers, though no breath moves through him. "Someone must tell him. Someone must survive telling him."
>
> The spirit vanishes into the dark below.
>
> The crystals along the stair answer with a slow pulse.
>
> Not a direction.
>
> An invitation.
>
> Or a warning.

## 7. Player Curiosity Hooks

### Can We Explore the City?

Not broadly in Chapter 7. The route is unstable, blocked, and spiritually disorienting. The Tremorscope and spirit movement make Bridgeward the meaningful path.

### Are the Spirits Hostile?

No, not by default. They are witnesses and echoes. Violence against them should feel morally wrong and may worsen resonance.

### Did Someone Kill the Queen?

No. The Queen's Bridge memory should make clear that the fall was caused by a tremor.

### Are the Crystals Alive?

Not in a normal sense. They are grief-resonance growths. Some have become malignant enough to move, such as the Witness-Hall Grief-Crystal Animus and the Crystal Shardlings.

### Is the King Controlling the Crystal Creatures?

No. His grief created the conditions, but he does not command them.

### Is The Hollow Below Part of the Curse?

No. It is a foreign ancient predator that nested beneath the bridge and feeds on the wound.

### Where Does the Path Lead?

Eventually downward toward the throne room and confrontation with the king. Chapter 7 stops before that descent becomes the active scene.

## 8. Escalation Signal

Use these to push the party forward:

- The Tremorscope beat becomes synchronized with crystals along the route.
- Spirits increasingly move in one direction: Bridgeward.
- Erny and Maelreth hear the hum behind their teeth become almost like a second pulse.
- The city overlook reveals no safe alternate route.
- A distant bell-memory sounds once from the bridge, then cuts off mid-ring.
