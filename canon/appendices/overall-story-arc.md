# Appendix — Overall Story Arc: The Grieving King of Stillpeak

## Canon Purpose

This appendix defines the locked campaign-level story arc for Stillpeak's Grieving Heart and provides the foundation for future interior packets, beginning with Packet 6.

It should be treated as supporting canon for the module's deep-lore spine, especially when designing clues, interior locations, memory fragments, final-act encounters, and resolution logic.

---

## Core Truth

Stillpeak is not evil.

Stillpeak is a neutral, ancient, natural mountain that has become receptive to the unresolved grief of an immortal dwarven king buried within it.

The source of the mountain's grief is an ancient dwarven king who lost his queen during a ceremonial crossing inside the mountain. A natural tremor shook the bridge during the rite, and the queen fell into the depths to her death.

The original tragedy was not murder, betrayal, or malice. It was a natural mountain event that the king could not accept.

---

## The King's Corruption

After the queen's death, the king refused release. He sought to preserve her, recover her, hear her, reverse the moment, or remain with her in some impossible way.

Some unknown power, royal mourning rite, resonance engine, or mountain-deep force made him practically immortal.

His immortality did not heal him. It trapped him at the emotional instant of loss.

Over centuries, he developed an uncanny ability to channel and attune himself to Stillpeak's deep-earth resonance. His grief, rage, and despair turned the mountain into an amplifier for his unresolved sorrow.

He does not control the mountain like a wizard controls a spell.

He grieves through it.

His sorrow gives the mountain rhythm. His rage gives it pressure. His madness gives it rules.

---

## The Dwarven Kingdom and Exodus

Stillpeak once contained or crowned an ancient dwarven seat or mountain-kingdom.

After the queen's death, the king withdrew deeper into mourning and resonance. The court, priests, geomancers, engineers, and loyal retainers attempted to stabilize the king, the bridge site, and the mountain's worsening interior conditions.

The kingdom did not flee in a single instant. The fall occurred in stages:

1. **The Fall:** The queen dies during the ceremonial bridge crossing.
2. **The Sealing:** The king withdraws inward; the court attempts to stabilize the mountain and contain the grief-resonance.
3. **The Exodus:** Surviving dwarves abandon the mountain after tremors, impossible mourning phenomena, preserved dead, failing passages, and the king's refusal to leave or release others make the kingdom untenable.

Recommended working chronology:

- Queen's death: roughly 400 years ago.
- Dwarven exodus: roughly 380-390 years ago.
- Long contained haunting: centuries of localized danger, failed ascents, and old rescue tragedies.
- Recent reactivation: 3 months ago.
- Cinderwatch crisis: roughly 2 months ago.
- Party involvement: now.

These dates are approximate design anchors unless later canon requires exact calendar placement.

---

## Why the Crisis Became Recent

The grief is ancient, but the current crisis is recent because the containment failed or the old wound reactivated.

Three months ago, a natural deep tremor struck the same resonance fault, chamber, bridge structure, or mountain-heart alignment involved in the queen's death.

The tremor did not create the grief.

It replayed the original wound.

The king experienced the new tremor as the queen's fall happening again. His immortal grief surged, old containment cracked, and Stillpeak began broadcasting his sorrow through the mountain instead of keeping it localized within the sealed upper/interior zones.

This explains why Southwatch has tracked the rhythmic pattern for three months, why Cinderwatch's visible crisis emerged after the pulse began spreading, and why older failed climbs can still be true without implying the full regional crisis has always existed.

---

## Long History of Failed Climbs

Before the recent escalation, Stillpeak was already dangerous, but its grief-field was more localized and cyclical.

Older failed climbs, dead rescuers, abandoned route markers, old trailwarden traditions, and high-slope disappearances were contact incidents with the edge of the wound, not full outbreak events.

The old grief-field was strongest near:

- old dwarven entrances
- high approach routes
- the summit face
- rescue paths
- interior bridge remnants
- places symbolically tied to burden, crossing, descent, and failed rescue

This is why guides, trail keepers, marker workers, rescuers, climbers, and stone-workers are thematically important. They create passage, carry people downward, brace bridges, mark routes, and oppose the king's unresolved moment.

The mountain's grief does not merely kill them. It keeps them, freezes them, repeats them, or turns them into evidence that passage and rescue failed.

## How the Existing Exterior Clues Point to the Truth

The exterior adventure has been teaching the king's wound before the party knows his name.

### The Tremorscope

The Tremorscope does not detect evil. It listens to the repeating wound.

Its increasing signal reflects proximity to the grief-source. At the summit face, it answers from inside the stone because the party has reached the threshold between symptoms and source.

### The Karagvorn Line

The Karagvorn Line is the point where the king's grief-resonance begins reinterpreting reality.

Magic is not simply suppressed. Tools still work, but stop helping. Direction, burden, storage, sound, distance, and motion become technically correct in ways that make survival harder.

### Burden Logic

Burden matters because the queen died during a ceremonial crossing and because the king's mind has turned crossing, carrying, and balance into moral laws.

The mountain measures load, hesitation, rescue, and passage because the king cannot stop replaying the bridge.

### The Dead Who Do Not Fall

The dead remain upright, preserved, or unfinished because the king refuses completion. Death, descent, and release are unacceptable to him.

### Failed Rescuers and Old Route Markers

Rescuers and route-makers are punished or preserved because they symbolically challenge the king's grief-state: they create ways down, retrieve the lost, mark paths, and prevent the mountain from keeping what it has claimed.

### The Summit Door

The summit is not the destination. It is the threshold into the grief-source.

The concealed dwarven door leads inward and downward, toward the old wound rather than toward a simple exterior peak.

## Interior Design Direction

Future interior packets should reveal the truth gradually through environmental clues, memory fragments, dwarven records, resonance events, and symbolic spaces.

Do not reveal the full story immediately.

Recommended clue progression:

1. The interior is dwarven and ceremonial, not merely defensive.
2. A bridge, crossing, or royal rite was central to the catastrophe.
3. The queen fell during a natural tremor.
4. The king survived, refused release, and withdrew deeper.
5. The kingdom tried to contain him and then fled.
6. The recent tremor replayed the original death and broke containment.
7. The mountain's current horrors are grief-patterns amplified through Stillpeak.
8. The final problem is the attunement between king, grief, and mountain-heart.

Interior locations should emphasize:

- preserved halls that refuse time
- broken bridges and impossible drops
- ceremonial spaces turned into mourning engines
- dwarven engineering records and evacuation marks
- dead held in unfinished purpose
- resonance chambers where emotion alters terrain
- memory strata showing incomplete or contradictory versions of the queen's death
- evidence that the king's grief is tragic but catastrophically harmful

---

## Final-Act Principle

The final conflict should not be solved by simply killing a villain.

The king is tragic, but he is not absolved. His refusal to mourn has harmed the living, trapped the dead, and forced a neutral mountain to express his rage and despair.

The party's ultimate task should be to break the attunement between the immortal king's unresolved sorrow and Stillpeak's heart.

Possible resolution vectors include:

- restoring the true memory of the queen's death as accident rather than betrayal
- proving that release is not abandonment
- severing or retuning the resonance engine / royal mourning rite
- allowing the mountain to return to neutrality
- laying the queen properly to rest
- forcing the king to experience grief as mortal sorrow again, not geological law

Combat may occur, but combat alone should not be the whole answer.

---

## Tone Rule

The king should not become sympathetic too early.

The party should first experience the harm caused by his grief: Cinderwatch's suffering, failed rescuers, trapped dead, distorted reality, and the mountain's violence.

Sympathy should arrive later as complication, not absolution.

The king is a grieving figure who became a catastrophe.
